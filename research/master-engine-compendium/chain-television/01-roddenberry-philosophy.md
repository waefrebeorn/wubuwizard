## Core Facts

- **Gene Roddenberry** (1921-1991). Born in El Paso, Texas. Served in US Army Air Corps in WWII (15 combat missions as B-17 pilot). Later LAPD police officer. Became writer-producer in television.
- **Star Trek** (1966-1969, NBC, 3 seasons, 79 episodes). Created by Roddenberry. The show that defined televised science fiction as a vehicle for social commentary.
- **Roddenberry's philosophy**: "Vision of the future" — a hopeful, humanist 1960s vision where humanity had put aside racism, poverty, and war. Science and rational thinking were central. He refused depictions of religion as positive force. His future was one where humanity widely embraced reason and rejected mysticism.

## Roddenberry as Television Auteur

- Roddenberry propagated a narrative of himself as a "visionary" writer-producer and primary author of Star Trek.
- He wrote or co-wrote 11 TOS episodes directly: "The Cage," "Charlie X," "Mudd's Women," "The Menagerie," "The Return of the Archons," "A Private Little War," "The Omega Glory," "Bread and Circuses," "Assignment: Earth," "The Savage Curtain," "Turnabout Intruder."
- He also wrote TNG's "Encounter at Farpoint," "Hide and Q," and "Datalore."
- The Star Trek Writers Guild arbitration (The Menagerie rewrite) is evidence of his contested authorship — he rewrote footage from "The Cage" into "The Menagerie," resulting in a WGA hearing about who deserved credit.
- His vision: Star Trek as a utopian future where humanity had solved its problems — but the episodes often dealt with contemporary social issues (racism, Cold War, gender equality) through allegory.
