# NODE 26: Hunter S. Thompson — Gonzo Journalism, "Fear and Loathing", Writing Method, Extreme Life Philosophy

## Core concept
Hunter S. Thompson = the master engine's WRITING VOICE: gonzo journalism, first-person subjective, drug-fueled stream of consciousness, the writer as participant, satire + social commentary + chaos. The engine's commentary mode should channel HST.

## Key facts

### Gonzo journalism defined
- "Gonzo journalism" Thompson pioneered — a mix of satire, dark humor, parody, social commentary
- "drug-fueled stream of consciousness writing technique" (Wikipedia)
- Thompson's own 1975 interview explains what gonzo is (Facebook EricAlperPR, Instagram @mrdavegram)
- "Are not the particular subjective filters by which facts and events are processed and imagined in a moment in history as relevant as the facts themselves in understanding the truth of that moment, or at least a slice of the truth?" (Open Culture)
- Gonzo = the particular subjective filters are AS RELEVANT as the facts themselves

### Thompson's method
- Self-imposed training regime: re-typed the whole of _A Farewell to Arms_ and _The Great Gatsby_ word for word — a method for learning how to write like the masters (Open Culture)
- "He had a healthy respect for structure, cultivated through his early career in workaday reportage" (Open Culture)
- The drugs/chaos were NOT the main ingredient — structure + craft were the foundation
- "When the going gets weird, the weird turn pro" — Thompson's operating principle

### Key works
- _Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas_ (1971) — first serialized in Rolling Stone; the culmination of a particular writing project
- _Fear and Loathing on the Campaign Trail '72_ — political gonzo
- "We were somewhere around Barstow on the edge of the desert when the drugs began to take hold." — the most famous opening in gonzo
- The "Duke" persona — Thompson's larger-than-life character that blurred the lines between his work, public persona, and true self
- From late 1970s onward: "the lines between his larger-than-life character in his work, his public persona, and his true self began to blur" (Open Culture)

### Influences + lineage
- Tom Wolfe: "manic, highly adrenal first-person style in which Thompson's own emotions continually dominate the story" (IALJS PDF)
- New Journalism: influenced by the shift toward personal exuberance and subject, Whitmanic self-confidence of Kerouac (Allen Ginsberg Project)
- Thompson adapted New Journalists' techniques + added his own = Gonzo
- "The 'Most Accurate, Least Factual' Writer" — Thompson's paradox (ECU PDF)
- Key editors: Jann Wenner (Rolling Stone), Byron Dobell (Esquire), Warren Hinckle — the editor-writer relationship was crucial
- "Thompson's letters provide much more... Curiously, Thompson expressed only a cursory interest in the concept of the New Journalism" (UF thesis)
- Fitzgerald: "no second acts in American lives" — Thompson's guiding belief

### The extreme life philosophy
- "The Terrible Paradox of Being a Creative Person" — Pursuit of Wonder video
- Thompson's daily routine: "harrowing, chemical-filled" (Open Culture)
- "He was not merely the character he portrayed in his work and public life, but the man who cared enough, and was talented enough, to create this character in order to explore, understand, and represent a very nuanced condition of the world during his time" (Open Culture)
- Died by suicide at 67, with a gun, at the moment of his own choosing — "a highly American manner"

### What Tom Wolfe said
- "Tom Wolfe shares a typical night out with Hunter S. Thompson" (TIME YouTube)
- Wolfe + Thompson = the two most prolific and revolutionary writers in New Journalism
- Both recognized by journalism/literary historians as central to the development of the movement
