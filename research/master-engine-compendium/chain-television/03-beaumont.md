## Core Facts

- **Charles Beaumont** (1929-1967). Born in Chicago, raised in Los Angeles. Died at 38 from a brain disease (unspecified in most sources — likely Alzheimer's-like degenerative condition).
- **Wrote 22 episodes of The Twilight Zone** — second only to Rod Serling (92 episodes). Beaumont was "responsible for some of the finest" TZ episodes per the Rod Serling interviews archive.
- **Mentored by Ray Bradbury** — "unquestionably the biggest influence on Beaumont's writing career." Bradbury saw "firsthand how rapidly Beaumont's craftsmanship with words and evocative storytelling progressed." Bradbury and Beaumont were part of the same L.A. writers circle that also included Richard Matheson.
- **The plagiarism question**: Did Rod Serling plagiarize Ray Bradbury? The Bunk History article addresses this — the question is about whether Serling borrowed Bradbury's themes/stories without credit. Beaumont was "the center of a circle of writers which included Ray Bradbury and Richard Matheson."
- **Ghostwriters**: Some Beaumont TZ episodes "required uncredited ghostwriters" per Syfy Wire. As Beaumont's health declined, he received creative help from uncredited writers on numerous episodes.

## Key Twilight Zone Episodes by Beaumont

Season 1 (1959-1960):
- "Perchance to Dream" (S1E9) — a man terrified of dreaming because his dreams are fatal. Written by Beaumont. Directed by Robert Florey.
- "Elegy" (S1E20) — three men arrive at a cemetery and discover they are the corpses in the graves. Written by Beaumont. Directed by Douglas Heyes.
- "Long Live Walter Jameson" (S1E24) — an old man claims to have been alive for centuries. Written by Beaumont. Directed by Anton Leader.
- "A Nice Place to Visit" (S1E28) — a criminal dies and thinks he's in heaven, but it's hell. Written by Beaumont. Directed by John Brahm.

Season 2 (1960-1961):
- "The Lateness of the Hour" (S2E1) — a roboticist's household robots begin acting strangely. Written by Beaumont.
- "The Night of the Meek" (S2E2) — a department store Santa discovers real magic on Christmas. Written by Beaumont.
- "The Whole Truth" (S2E4) — a car dealer buys a car that forces him to tell the truth. Written by Beaumont.
- "Twenty Two" (S2E5) — a dancer keeps dreaming of a plane crash that then happens in reality. Written by Beaumont.
- "Static" (S2E6) — a man can hear radio broadcasts from his dead wife through his radio. Written by Beaumont.
- "Long Distance Call" (S2E7) — a boy communicates with his dead grandmother through a toy telephone. Written by Beaumont.

Season 3 (1961-1962):
- "The Howling Man" (S3E5) — travelers find a man who may be the Devil, imprisoned by a priest. Written by Beaumont. One of the most famous TZ episodes.
- "The Mirror" (S3E12) — a man discovers a mirror that shows him as he truly is. Written by Beaumont.
- "The Grave" (S3E17) — a surly old man refuses to be buried until his horse returns from the dead. Written by Beaumont.
- "Person or Persons Unknown" (S3E28) — a woman's husband slowly loses his identity as everyone forgets him. Written by Beaumont.

Season 4 (1963-1964):
- "No Time Like the Past" (S4E10) — a time traveler goes back to prevent WWII but can't change anything. Written by Beaumont.
- "The Bard" (S4E17) — a failing playwright summons Shakespeare for help, but Shakespeare is petty and cruel. Written by Beaumont.

## WuBu Relevance

Beaumont's episodes are the most psychologically eerie of TZ — "Perchance to Dream" (fear of sleep), "The Howling Man" (the Devil imprisoned in a cell), "Person or Persons Unknown" (loss of identity). The WuBuMovie's liminal storage units + the Order + the Old UNC's deadpan stare maps directly onto Beaumont's aesthetic: ordinary settings + psychological dread + a twist that reveals the character's own nature. Beaumont also proves the "ghostwriter" problem — when the creator's health fails, the quality drops. WuBuMovie's "AGI as creator" is the same risk: the AGI is the Beaumont figure, but the AGI doesn't get sick. The risk is different: the AGI doesn't get better either.
