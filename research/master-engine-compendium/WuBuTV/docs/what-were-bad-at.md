## 1. COMEDY TIMING IN VIDEO (We Don't Know This)

From research: The three pillars of comedy timing are:
- **The Pause**: Silence before the punchline. The audience needs time to process the setup.
- **Rule of Three**: Two normal items, third breaks the pattern. In video = two normal shots, third is the joke.
- **Misdirection**: Camera/attention goes one direction, joke happens elsewhere.

**What we do wrong**: We generate 5-second clips with no timing. No pause before the punchline. No setup-payoff structure.

**What we need to learn**:
- How long to hold a shot before cutting (the "comedy beat")
- When to cut AWAY from the joke (reaction shot vs. direct shot)
- How to use silence (not just audio) for comedic effect
- The "straight face" technique: character doesn't react, audience laughs

## 2. POST-PRODUCTION (We Barely Know This)

From FilmLocal research, professional post-production has:

### Picture Editing
1. Assembly cut (chronological)
2. Rough cut (trim + structure)
3. Director's cut (vision)
4. Fine cut (polish)
5. **Picture lock** (NO MORE CHANGES)

### Audio Post (AFTER picture lock)
1. Dialog editing (noise reduction, normalization to -11dB to -10dB)
2. Foley (footsteps, cloth, props — recorded separately)
3. Sound design (ambient, atmosphere)
4. Music editing
5. Final mix

### Visual Polish
1. Color correction (fix exposure/white balance)
2. Color grading (stylistic look)
3. VFX compositing
4. Titles/graphics

**What we do wrong**: We generate a clip and call it done. No editing. No audio post. No color.

**What we need to learn**:
- ffmpeg editing (cuts, transitions, pacing)
- Audio mixing (dialog + music + SFX at proper levels)
- Color grading (LUTs, curves, film look)
- Sound design (ambient beds, foley, SFX layering)

## 3. VISUAL STORYTELLING (We Don't Know This)

**What we do wrong**: Single shot, static, no visual variety.

**What we need to learn**:
- Shot types (wide, medium, close-up, POV)
- Camera movement (push in, pan, tilt, tracking)
- The Kuleshov effect (meaning comes from juxtaposition of shots)
- Montage (compressing time through cuts)

## 4. SOUND DESIGN (We Don't Know This)

From FilmLocal: "Audio quality sets apart amateur productions from professional ones."

**The audio stack** (in order of priority):
1. **Dialog** — must be clear, normalized to -11dB to -10dB
2. **Foley** — footsteps, cloth movement, object handling
3. **Ambient** — room tone, environment (fluorescent hum, AC drone)
4. **SFX** — specific sounds for actions/events
5. **Music** — emotional underscore

**What we do wrong**: We either have no audio or just music. No layering. No ambient bed. No foley.

**What we need to learn**:
- How to create an ambient bed (looping background sound)
- When to use silence vs. sound
- How music interacts with dialog (ducking, crossfade)
- Sound perspective (close sounds vs. far sounds)

---

## 5. THE FOUND FOOTAGE AESTHETIC (We Fake This)

Real found footage isn't just "add grain and chromatic aberration." It's:
- **Camera behavior**: Handheld shake, zoom adjustments, focus hunting
- **Temporal artifacts**: Timecode, recording glitches, tape damage
- **Framing mistakes**: Partially cut-off subjects, awkward angles
- **Lighting reality**: Overexposed windows, underexposed faces
- **Sound reality**: Wind noise, handling noise, automatic gain pumping

**What we do wrong**: We add a VHS filter and call it found footage.

**What we need to learn**:
- What makes footage feel "real" vs. "rendered"
- How to simulate camera behavior (not just static)
- How to create temporal artifacts that feel authentic
- When to break the "rules" of good cinematography (on purpose)
