## The Four Steps
### 1. Premise (find the emotional truth)
Something real — a feeling, frustration, contradiction. Doesn't have to be
funny; has to be *true*. (Pull from [[PREMISE_BANK]].)
- e.g. "Game shows are rigged." "Storage units are creepy at night."

### 2. Comedy Lens (your "what if")
Your specific angle. Two people with the same premise get different jokes
because their lenses differ.
- Premise: "Game shows are rigged."
- Lens A: Old UNC as a host who *knows* it's rigged.
- Lens B: all the prizes are terrible, everyone pretends they're great.
- (Build your own in [[LENS_LIBRARY]].)

### 3. Norm (establish the misdirection)
The "normal" you break. Straighter = harder twist. In 10s, set it in 0-3s.
- Old UNC walks a hallway. Normal. Vending machines. Normal. He's in his hoody. Normal.

### 4. Twist (the benign violation)
Breaks the norm — *surprising but not threatening*. Sweet spot: "that
shouldn't be there, but I'm glad it is." His deadpan makes it benign.
- Hallway breathes; he adjusts his pace and sighs.

## Blueprint → 10-Second Map
- Premise = the world (from [[UNIVERSE]])
- Lens = the scene choice (channel format)
- Norm = seconds 0-3
- Twist = seconds 7-10

## Pitfalls
- Premise too broad ("life is hard" ≠ premise).
- Lens too obvious (first idea = cliché; push to 4th/5th).
- Norm not established (no incongruity).
- Twist not benign (disturbed ≠ amused — pull back).
