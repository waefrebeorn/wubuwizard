## Core Concept

Fawlty Towers (1975-1979) is the greatest escalation comedy ever made. 12 episodes. Each one: a small problem becomes a catastrophe through a series of compounding mistakes. John Cleese's Basil Fawlty is the prototype for the Old UNC — a character who tries to maintain dignity in an absurd world.

## The Escalation Architecture

Every Fawlty Towers episode follows the same structure:
1. **The small problem**: Something minor goes wrong (a car won't start, a guest complains)
2. **The cover-up**: Basil tries to hide the problem, making it worse
3. **The compounding**: Each attempt to fix the problem creates a new problem
4. **The explosion**: Everything collapses at once
5. **The aftermath**: Basil stands in the wreckage, dignity intact

## The WuBu Escalation

The ~ WuBu ~ bumper uses the same architecture, compressed into 10 seconds:
1. **0:00–0:02 (Small problem)**: The hallway is breathing
2. **0:02–0:05 (Cover-up)**: The Old UNC tries to ignore it
3. **0:05–0:08 (Compounding)**: The breathing gets louder, the walls move
4. **0:08–0:10 (Explosion/Aftermath)**: He sighs, continues walking

## John Cleese's Rules

John Cleese (co-creator and star) established rules for escalation comedy:
- **The character must be serious** — Basil never knows he's funny. The Old UNC never knows he's funny.
- **Each mistake must be logical** — Basil's mistakes make sense in the moment. The Old UNC's reactions make sense.
- **The stakes must be real to the character** — Basil genuinely cares about his hotel's reputation. The Old UNC genuinely... doesn't care. That's the inversion.
- **The audience must be ahead of the character** — The audience sees the catastrophe coming. The character doesn't.

## Pitfalls
- **Too fast** — Fawlty Towers takes 30 minutes to escalate. A bumper has 10 seconds. Compress, don't rush.
- **No logic** — Each escalation step must make sense. If the hallway breathing leads to a chicken appearing, there must be a logical (absurd) connection.
- **The character cares too much** — Basil cares desperately. The Old UNC doesn't care at all. The comedy comes from his non-reaction to the escalating absurdity.
