## Core Concept

The Harold is the foundational longform improv structure, developed by Del Close and Charna Halpern at the Upright Citizens Brigade. It's a structure for creating coherent comedy from nothing — and it maps perfectly onto the ~ WuBu ~ bumper format.

## The Harold Structure

The Harold is performed in three beats:
1. **First Beat**: Three unrelated scenes (A, B, C) — each with its own premise
2. **Group Game**: A scene involving all performers — establishes themes
3. **Second Beat**: The three scenes return (A2, B2, C2) — each heightens its premise
4. **Group Game**: Another group scene — deepens themes
5. **Third Beat**: The three scenes converge (ABC) — all premises connect

## The Harold Applied to 10-Second Bumpers

The Harold structure maps onto a bumper:
1. **0:00–0:03 (Scene A)**: Establish the world (Old UNC in hallway)
2. **0:03–0:06 (Scene B)**: Introduce the absurd element (hallway breathes)
3. **0:06–0:08 (Group Game)**: The two elements collide (Old UNC reacts to breathing hallway)
4. **0:08–0:10 (Scene C/Convergence)**: The punchline (he sighs, continues walking)

## Del Close's Rules

Del Close (the godfather of modern improv) established rules that apply to comedy writing:
- **"Yes, and"**: Accept what came before and build on it
- **"Make your partner look good"**: Every beat should give the next beat something to work with
- **"Don't force it"**: Let the comedy emerge from the situation, don't impose it
- **"The game of the scene"**: Find the one funny thing and explore it

## The UCB Connection

The Upright Citizens Brigade (UCB) is the comedy school that trained:
- Amy Poehler, Tina Fey, Kate McKinnon (SNL)
- Donald Glover, Ed Helms, Ellie Kemper (film/TV)
- The entire modern sketch comedy generation

Their approach: find the "game" of the scene (the one funny thing), then heighten it through escalation. This is exactly the ~ WuBu ~ bumper structure: find the one funny thing (height opposition, breathing hallway, existential game show), then heighten it.

## Pitfalls
- **Too many premises** — The Harold uses three scenes because it's a 30-minute show. A 10-second bumper uses ONE game. Don't try to cram three premises into 10 seconds.
- **No heightening** — The Harold's power is in the second and third beats, where premises heighten. A bumper needs the same: the second half must be bigger than the first.
- **Forgetting the group game** — The group beat is where themes deepen. In a bumper, this is the moment where the character reacts to the absurdity.
