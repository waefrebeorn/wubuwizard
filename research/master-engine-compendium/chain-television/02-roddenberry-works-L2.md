## The PAX Trilogy (1973-1975)

Roddenberry's post-Trek attempts to create a new series all failed the pilot stage but reveal his evolving vision:

- **Genesis II** (1973, NBC pilot). Starred Alex Cord as Dylan Hunt, a scientist awakened from suspended animation in a post-apocalyptic future. Director: John Llewellyn Moxey. The premise: Hunt leads a team from "Gene's World" (a technologically advanced underground bunker) to explore the surface, which has regressed to tribal societies. The pilot was well-received but NBC passed — too similar to Star Trek, they said. Mariette Hartley played a Tyranian defector who explained the worldbuilding.

- **Planet Earth** (1974, NBC pilot). A reworked version of Genesis II with a female lead (Marlena Eve Roberts, played by..?). The premise was similar but with a different cast. Also rejected.

- **Strange New World** (1975, ABC pilot, also called "The Stranger"). Yet another rework of the same concept — Dylan Hunt's team exploring post-apocalyptic Earth. Also rejected.

The PAX Trilogy represents Roddenberry's inability to escape his own success — every post-Trek project was "Star Trek but different," and the networks rejected them for being too similar OR too weird.

## The Questor Tapes (1974)

- TV movie pilot starring Robert Foxworth as Questor, an advanced android who discovers he has emotions. Written by Roddenberry AND Gene L. Coon (the TOS writer most associated with developing the Trek universe — "The Menagerie," "The Savage Curse," etc.). The script "was written by both Roddenberry and the writer/producer most often credited with shaping the best of TOS Star Trek" — meaning Coon shaped the story while Roddenberry shaped the vision.

- The Questor Tapes is significant because it introduced the android-with-emotions concept that directly inspired Data in Star Trek: The Next Generation. Majel Barrett Roddenberry (Roddenberry's wife, who voiced the computer on TOS/TNG) has said that Questor was the prototype for Data. The android's struggle to understand humanity is the same arc Data undergoes in TNG.

- The pilot was not picked up as a series, but its DNA lived on in Data.

## Spectre (1977)

- Supernatural detective series starring Nicol Williamson as John Spectre, a psychiatrist who sees ghosts. Directed by Robert Scheerer. This was Roddenberry's attempt to fuse sci-fi with supernatural horror — essentially, a "Twilight Zone" detective show. It was not picked up as a series.

- Spectre is relevant to WuBuMovie because it's the closest Roddenberry came to the "found footage / supernatural investigation" genre. A host figure (Spectre) investigating the paranormal, one case per episode. The WuBuMovie's Old UNC is essentially a Spectre figure — a lead character investigating the liminal/overdetermined storage unit world.

## Earth: Final Conflict (1997-2001)

- Roddenberry's FIRST NON-TREK SUCCESS. 5 seasons, 78 episodes, syndicated. Based on a plot outline Roddenberry wrote before his death in 1991. The series was produced posthumously by his son and Majel Barrett Roddenberry. Starred Kevin Conway as Jake Weaver, a resistance fighter against the Taelons (aliens who appear benevolent but are actually manipulating humanity).

- The Taelons are the key concept: they appear to be offering humanity paradise (cures for diseases, advanced technology) but they're actually harvesting human genetic material or manipulating human evolution. The show was a mix of conspiracy thriller and sci-fi — closer to The X-Files than Star Trek.

- Despite running for 5 seasons, the show was "plagued by setbacks" — production issues, inconsistent scripts, declining quality. It's remembered as a cult item but not a breakout hit.

## Andromeda (2000-2005)

- 5 seasons, 110 episodes, syndicated. Starred Kevin Sorbo (of Hercules fame) as Captain Dylan Hunt, reviving the character from Genesis II. Based on Roddenberry's original concept. Produced by Majel Barrett Roddenberry after her husband's death. The show was more action-oriented than Star Trek — "Star Trek on steroids" — with more physical conflict, fewer philosophical musings.

- Andromeda is significant for WuBuMovie because it's the most "Roddenberry" of his non-Trek shows — same Dylan Hunt character, same exploration-of-worlds premise, but with a different tone. It proves Roddenberry's core concept (a team exploring worlds) was durable across 30+ years.

## Unproduced Projects

- **Star Trek: Phase II** (1977) — planned as a TV movie leading to a new series. Would have introduced the characters of The Motion Picture but with a new cast. Cancelled when Star Wars success caused Paramount to greenlight The Motion Picture theatrical film instead. The Phase II scripts were recycled into the first two TMP sequels (TWOK, The Search for Spock).

- **Star Trek: The God Thing** — unproduced concept, no details available beyond the title.

## WuBu Relevance (Leg 2)

Roddenberry's post-Trek career teaches WuBuMovie three lessons:
1. The "team exploring worlds" template is durable but needs a twist to not feel like Trek copy: WuBuMovie's twist is the found-footage/liminal storage unit setting. The "team" is a single character (Old UNC) walking through storage units. The "worlds" are different rooms/ceremonies.
2. The android-with-emotions arc (Questor → Data) is a reusable character concept: WuBuMovie's Old UNC could be an "android with emotions" figure — a character discovering feelings in a world of objects. Or the opposite: a human discovering he's in a world of objects.
3. Spectre is the closest Roddenberry got to the WuBuMovie format: a host-investigator, one case per episode, supernatural/liminal elements. WuBuMovie's episodes could be structured as "The Tale of Storage Unit 7-B" or "The Tale of the Teal Orb."
