## WHY THESE SEVEN SHARE RIGS ALREADY BANKED

**PT-9…PT-12 re-use the Puppet rig verbatim** — one cartoon skeleton, pale-blue
visor, white paper apron, clearly drawn skull, full ribcage, visible finger bones,
locked-off medium, dry room tone, and the identity lock restated in the canon
block every time. **P-7…P-9 re-use the Public Access rig** — green couch, plain
carpet, one short woman, one heavyset man in a plain black hoodie, locked-off wide,
and a laugh track at one or two fixed points.

**That is the point of writing to a rig rather than inventing per episode: four
more puppets cost no new continuity surface, and three more community tapes cost
no new set.** Fire PT-9…PT-12 from the PT seed, P-7…P-9 from the P seed, and
6-tile the cast across each group.

## FOUR THINGS FLAGGED, NOT DISCOVERED

1. **PT-10 is the props class again, and it is the worst instance yet.** The
   premise is a blank lesson card, and I have written it as *one plain white
   rectangle with a clean even surface* — but a **card is a thing a model fills.**
   That is the all-lit tubes, the unplugged board, the blank counter, the blank
   lanyard card and the empty corner, all over again, and this one is worse because
   the blankness is the entire subject. If any writing appears on that card the
   episode is dead. If it does, do not re-ask: strengthen the positive to a card
   with a **visible printed border rule and a clean unmarked interior field**, so
   the model has a surface it can treat as a document without filling.

2. **P-7, P-8 and P-9 share the height gag, so they share one failure mode.** The
   model will want to *fix* the geometry — crouch him, put her on a box, draw her
   taller. All three break if it does, and P-8 is the sharpest version because both
   figures are **seated**: it is only their head and shoulder levels that carry the
   joke, and a model that seats them at similar heights has destroyed the episode
   while making it look balanced. 6-tile the two figures together in every tile.

3. **PT-11's encore depends on the visored skeleton holding absolutely still** for
   24 seconds while two others move. That is a long static hold in a stop-motion
   clip, which is exactly where a generator adds idle motion — a small head turn, a
   breathing bob, a hand drift. **Any motion from the visored skeleton breaks the
   episode**, because the joke is that the one who has not moved is the one who
   intended to. Tile all three across the clip.

4. **P-9 inverts the News rig, so it needs its own tile.** A tripod camera pointed
   at a couch is a *camera* inside the frame, and the episode's own locked-off
   camera plus that tripod are two different camera behaviours in one shot. Make
   certain the tripod holds one identical aim point for all 30s — if it drifts or
   re-aims, the "coverage" reads as an event and the reversal is gone.
