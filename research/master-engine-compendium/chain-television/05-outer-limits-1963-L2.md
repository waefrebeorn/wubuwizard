## The "Control Voice" — In Detail

- The Control Voice was voiced by **Vic Perrin** (not Joseph Cotten — Cotten hosted the 1995 revival). Perrin was a prolific voice actor (he voiced many animated characters, including the narrator of "The Adventures of Superman" cartoons). His Control Voice was cold, mechanical, authoritative — the opposite of Rod Serling's warm, conversational style.
- The Control Voice's opening narration (the famous "There is nothing wrong with your television set..." passage) was written by **Leslie Stevens** and **L.D. Salzman** (the story editor). It was designed to be a "framing device" that established the show's premise: the viewer is being controlled by an outside force. The "control" metaphor is central to the show's identity.
- The Control Voice also appeared at the end of each episode, with a variant of the opening narration: "You have just watched a film from the archives of the Control Voice. The Control Voice is always watching..." (or similar — there were multiple variants).
- The Control Voice is significant because it established the show's "anti-host" stance: unlike TZ's Serling, who was a warm, conversational guide, the Control Voice was a cold, mechanical, all-powerful entity. This is the WuBuMovie's ANGLE: the MEGA is the Control Voice. The Old UNC is the character who experiences it. The AGI is the entity that controls the transmission.

## Notable Episodes — Detailed

### "The Architects of Fear" (S1E11, 1963)
- Premise: A group of scientists, led by an anthropologist named Allen Leighton, create an alien creature (an "android" made of organic material) to appear in a major city. The goal: to frighten humanity into unity by presenting a common enemy. The alien is created from the body of a volunteer (a man named Allan Leighton, who agrees to be transformed into the alien). The alien appears in the city, and the plan works — humanity unites against the "alien threat." But the alien's creator's wife discovers the truth and tries to stop it. The alien's creator (Allan) has been transformed into the alien and is dying. The episode ends with the alien's death and the revelation of the conspiracy.
- Why it works: The episode is a commentary on the "common enemy" trope in politics — humanity unites against an external threat. The "alien" is a manufactured threat, a conspiracy. The episode's twist: the "alien" is actually a human being (Allan Leighton) who volunteered to be transformed. The episode's theme: fear unites, but the cost is human life.
- WuBu parallel: The Order is the "common enemy" — a manufactured threat that unites the characters in the storage units. The Old UNC's role is the "creator" — he's the one who "created" the Order (or believes he did). The WuBuMovie's conspiracy: the Order is a manufactured threat. The "alien" is the Old UNC himself.

### "The Man Who Was Never Born" (S1E12, 1963)
- Premise: A woman named Jeanne develops a time machine and travels back to the past to save her husband, who died in a car accident. She arrives in the past and finds that her husband is still alive — but he's a different person. He's a man who has lived for centuries, and he's tired of life. He wants to die. Jeanne tries to convince him to return to the future with her, but he refuses. The episode ends with Jeanne returning to the future alone, and her husband dying in the past.
- Why it works: The episode is a twist on the time-travel genre. The "time traveler" is not the protagonist — the woman is. The "immortal man" is the antagonist. The episode's theme: the burden of immortality, the desire for death. The twist: the husband's immortality is a curse, not a gift.
- WuBu parallel: The WuBuMovie's time element — the Old UNC's "eternal" life in the storage units, the Order's "ancient" existence. The WuBuMovie's theme: the burden of eternity, the desire for an end.

### "O.B.I.T." (S1E6, 1963)
- Premise: A man named David pursues a woman named Nora, who is involved with a secret organization called O.B.I.T. (Office of Biological and Interdimensional Tracking). The organization is monitoring the boundary between dimensions. The episode is a spy thriller with a sci-fi twist — the "organization" is actually monitoring the boundary between our dimension and another dimension where the "enemy" lives.
- Why it works: The episode is a Cold War spy thriller with a sci-fi premise. The "organization" is a metaphor for the intelligence agencies of the Cold War. The episode's theme: the surveillance state, the loss of privacy, the fear of the "other dimension."
- WuBu parallel: The Order is the "organization" — a secret group monitoring the boundary between the ordinary world and the liminal storage unit world. The WuBuMovie's theme: the surveillance of the liminal, the monitoring of the threshold.

## L.D. Salzman's Role

- Salzman was the story editor for The Outer Limits. His role was to shape the stories, maintain the show's tone, and ensure the sci-fi premises were consistent. He worked closely with Leslie Stevens.
- Salzman's influence: he was responsible for the show's "serious" tone — the episodes are not campy, they're serious sci-fi. The Control Voice, the serious tone, the focus on science and technology — these are Salzman's contributions.
- Salzman's background: he was a writer/producer who had worked on other television series before The Outer Limits. His role as story editor was the "showrunner" role before the term existed — he was the person who shaped the show's identity.
