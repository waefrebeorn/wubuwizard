# THE FOUR REPLACEMENT PREMISES (PT-9, PT-12, PT-15, PT-17)

Written 2026-09-29 in response to WuBu's ruling.

**WuBu was right and I was wrong, on a fact I could have checked.** I proposed a
renumber on the stated premise that six Puppet episodes were shipped. There are
**eight** — PT-1…PT-8 — so the collision was real, my premise was false, and the
renumber would have papered over it.

**The convention, as ruled: PT-1…PT-8 are shipped history, immutable. New episodes
number from PT-9 up with no gaps. A number with a finished clip is a fact; scripts
renumber on collision, never the reverse.** That is the correct rule and it is now
in force. These four keep the numbers and take **new premises** — no renames.

Retired to banked-but-duplicate: PT-9 THE LACES, PT-12 THE NEW STUDENT,
PT-15 THE MUSIC, PT-17 THE WARM-UP.

## WHAT I GOT WRONG, AND WHY IT MATTERS MORE THAN THE FOUR EPISODES

I proposed a renumber on a premise I had not checked. **I read my own writing room,
saw six Puppet headers, and stated that as the shipped count** — when eight episodes
have finished clips. If WuBu had accepted the proposal, it would have renumbered
scripts around a false fact and buried the collision permanently.

**"A number with a finished clip is a fact; scripts renumber on collision, never the reverse."** That is the right rule and I will not argue it: it is the same principle as deriving counts from artefacts rather than maintaining them, which is the lesson of this entire run — and I broke it in the one place where the artefact was a rendered file rather than a document.

The convention is now written down and in force. PT-1…PT-8 are immutable history;
everything new counts up from PT-9 with no gaps.

## FOUR FLAGS

1. **PT-9's sign has a TEXT TIMELINE, not a text identity** — one line at 0s, two
   from 14s. This is the first Puppet prompt whose text *changes*, and it is the
   riskiest of the four: check the first line is **identical** in every frame it
   appears, and that no third line appears. 6-tile the sign specifically.

2. **PT-15 is the only prompt in the puppet channel with a receding subject and no
   camera move.** A generator that adds a slow push or a track destroys the joke
   immediately, because the gag is that he leaves and the corridor simply continues.
   The negative block names all three failure modes. Verify the vanishing point
   holds the centre in every tile.

3. **PT-12's whole joke is that two identical things are treated as ordinary.** The
   failure is *helpful variety* — a slightly different second cup, a different
   counter. 6-tile both cups against each other, not each against its own earlier
   frame, because the comparison that matters is **cup-to-cup at the same instant.**

4. **PT-17 is a props-class-adjacent risk without being one.** The stack must stay
   a tidy column and must not collapse, scatter or gain a lid. It is an absence of
   disturbance rather than an absence of an object, so it did not trip the gate —
   but the failure mode is the same family, and it is the only one of the four where
   the model is likely to *add* something to a stack rather than fill an emptiness.
