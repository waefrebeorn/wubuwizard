## Core Concept

Ben Jonson's "Comedy of Humours" (late 1500s-early 1600s) is the foundation of character-driven comedy. The principle: each character has a dominant trait (a "humour") that overrides all other aspects of their personality. The comedy comes from watching characters be dominated by their single trait.

## The Four Humours

Jonson's system (based on medieval medicine):
- **Sanguine** (blood): Optimistic, enthusiastic, social
- **Choleric** (yellow bile): Ambitious, leader-like, irritable
- **Melancholic** (black bile): Analytical, thoughtful, prone to sadness
- **Phlegmatic** (phlegm): Calm, peaceful, unemotional

Each character is dominated by one humour. Their every action is filtered through that trait. The comedy comes from the rigidity — they can't act against their nature.

## The WuBu Humours

The ~ WuBu ~ characters are Jonsonian humour characters:
- **The Old UNC**: Phlegmatic — calm, deadpan, unemotional. His every action is filtered through non-reaction.
- **The Teal Orb**: Choleric — ambitious, mysterious, powerful. Its every action is filtered through authority.
- **The Height Opposition Characters**: Sanguine (short characters, enthusiastic) vs Melancholic (tall character, the Old UNC, analytical).

## Application to 10-Second Bumpers

In a bumper, the humour character is established in the first 2 seconds:
- "The Old UNC stares at a breathing hallway" — the audience immediately knows his humour (phlegmatic). He will not react. That's the comedy.
- The humour is the setup. The absurdity is the event. The gap between them is the laugh.

## Pitfalls
- **Too rigid** — A humour character who NEVER breaks character is boring. The Old UNC's phlegmatic humour is his default, but the rare break (a sigh, a stare) is the comedy.
- **Too many humours** — A 10-second bumper has ONE humour. The Old UNC's. Other characters are foils.
- **Explaining the humour** — Don't have a character say "I'm very calm." The humour should be visible in every action.
