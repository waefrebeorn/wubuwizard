## The History of Visual Bootlegging

Bootlegging — evoking a known aesthetic without using the brand name — is as old as visual media itself. The practice has a rich history that informs the ~ WuBu ~ approach:

### Early Television (1950s-1960s)
- Shows evoked competitors' formats without naming them. A variety show could look like Ed Sullivan's without saying the name.
- The "legal-safe description" was born from this era: describe the format, not the show.

### Sketch Comedy (1970s-1990s)
- Saturday Night Live, Monty Python, and The Kids in the Hall built entire careers on evoking formats without naming them.
- "The Church Lady" evoked televangelists without naming Jerry Falwell.
- "Wayne's World" evoked public access TV without naming a specific show.

### The Internet Era (2000s-2010s)
- YouTube sketch comedy (Smosh, Epic Rap Battles of History) used bootleg grammar to evoke movies, games, and TV shows.
- "Epic Rap Battles" evokes historical figures without licensing — pure grammar description.

### The AI Era (2020s-present)
- AI image/video generation makes bootlegging both easier (you can generate any aesthetic) and harder (AI moderation flags trademarked content).
- The solution: describe visual grammar so precisely that the AI generates the aesthetic without needing the brand name.

## The Principles

1. **Grammar over brand** — Describe the visual grammar, not the show name.
2. **Specific over generic** — "Warm wood-paneled living room" not "a room."
3. **Original characters** — Create your own characters within the grammar.
4. **One inversion** — One element that subverts the format is the joke.
5. **Transformative** — The result must add something new, not just copy.
