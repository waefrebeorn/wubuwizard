## Core Facts

- **Tales from the Crypt** (1989-1996, HBO, 7 seasons, 93 episodes). An anthology horror series based on EC Comics' "Tales from the Crypt" and its sister comics "The Vault of Horror" and "The Haunt of Fear." The series was produced by **Ellen Wolff** (who also produced "Boy Meets World" and other family shows — the irony of a family show producer helming a horror anthology is noted by critics). The series was executive produced by **Michael Reaves** and **Randall Serializer** (the production team that shaped the series' tone).

- **The Crypt Keeper** was the host — an undead, pun-speaking cadaver who opened and closed each episode. The Crypt Keeper was played by **Nolan Howell** in the first season, **Richard Moll** (who also played the Judge on "The Critic") in seasons 2-3, and **John Kassir** (the voice of Mushu in Disney's Mulan) in seasons 4-7. Kassir's voice became the definitive Crypt Keeper voice — his pun-heavy, gravelly, menacing-but-comedic delivery is iconic.

- **The Crypt Keeper's puns:** The Crypt Keeper's puns were a defining feature of the series. The Crypt Keeper would deliver a pun at the start and end of each episode — "Hello, BOY-zos!" "Mummy's the word!" "I'm dying to meet you!" The puns were a deliberate choice by the writers to make the horror accessible — the Crypt Keeper's humor disarmed the audience before the horror hit. The puns were written by the writing staff (Ellen Woolfolk, Michael Reaves, Randall Serializer, Bruce Poe, Jeff Converse, James V. Hart, and others).

- **The tone:** Tales from the Crypt was horror-comedy — the Crypt Keeper's puns established the comedic tone, but the episodes themselves were often genuinely disturbing. The series was notable for its twists — many episodes ended with a twist that subverted the audience's expectations. The series was also notable for its willingness to show graphic violence and horror — HBO's lack of censorship allowed the series to be more graphic than network television horror anthologies.

- **The EC Comics connection:** EC Comics (Educational Comics) was the publisher of "Tales from the Crypt," "The Vault of Horror," and "The Haunt of Fear" in the 1950s. EC Comics was founded by **William Gaines** (who also published "Mad" magazine). EC Comics was the subject of a 1954 Senate subcommittee investigation into comic book violence — the "comic book 청문회" that led to the Comics Code Authority and the effective death of EC's horror line. The EC Comics history is the backstory of Tales from the Crypt — the series is a revival of a comic line that was killed by censorship.
