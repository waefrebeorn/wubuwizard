# Lilypad 22/25 — Ten 10-Second Scenes: Funny Things That Can Happen (The Big List)
# Domain: Scene Examples | Chain: writing-room-25 | Depth: Leg 1
# 7-step Kevin Bacon: 10-second scene → funny event → visual gag → misunderstanding → opposition → callback → anti-joke

## The Big List

These are ten specific 10-second scene ideas, each built from the writing room research. Each one has a clear joke, a clear engine, and a clear "funny thing that happens." These are proofs of concept — they show the toolkit in action.

---

### Scene 1: "The Door That Isn't" (Misdirection + Sight Gag)
**Format:** Found footage
**Engine:** Misdirection
**The funny thing:** The Old UNC reaches for a door handle. His hand passes through it. He tries again. Through again. He stares at the door. The door stares back (the handle is now a face). He nods and walks away.

**Joke type:** Misdirection (expected: he opens the door; got: the door is not a door)
**Structure:** 0-3s: reaches for handle. 3-6s: hand passes through twice. 7-9s: handle is a face. 10s: walks away.

---

### Scene 2: "The Game Show That Knows" (Contrast + Absurdity)
**Format:** Game show
**Engine:** Contrast
**The funny thing:** The Old UNC is the host. He reads a question card: "WHAT DO YOU WANT?" The contestant (a teal orb) says nothing. The Old UNC marks it correct. The scoreboard shows "∞ to 0." The audience (more orbs) cheers silently.

**Joke type:** Absurdity (the game show has no questions, no answers, infinite score)
**Structure:** 0-2s: game show set, Old UNC at podium. 2-5s: reads question. 5-7s: orb says nothing, marked correct. 8-10s: scoreboard, silent cheers.

---

### Scene 3: "The Vending Machine's Opinion" (Absurd + Act-Out)
**Format:** Found footage
**Engine:** Absurd
**The funny thing:** The Old UNC presses A1. The vending machine makes a sound like a sigh. He presses B2. Another sigh, deeper. He leans down to the coin return. A voice from the machine: "Just take the chicken." He takes the chicken. Walks away. Text: "He always takes the chicken."

**Joke type:** Absurdity + Act-Out (the machine has opinions; his compliance is the act-out)
**Structure:** 0-3s: presses buttons, machine sighs. 4-6s: leans down, machine speaks. 7-9s: takes chicken. 10s: text card.

---

### Scene 4: "The Height Opposition" (Contrast + Observation)
**Format:** Sitcom / Arena
**Engine:** Contrast
**The funny thing:** The Old UNC stands next to a very short character (the height opposition world). They're both looking at something off-camera. The short character steps onto a box. The Old UNC takes a step forward (now he's further from the camera, effectively shorter). The short character steps onto a taller box. The Old UNC takes another step forward. This continues until the Old UNC is essentially a dot in the distance. The short character is still in frame, satisfied.

**Joke type:** Contrast + Act-Out (height competition through perspective)
**Structure:** 0-2s: both looking off-camera. 2-5s: box escalation. 5-9s: Old UNC receding. 10s: short character satisfied.

---

### Scene 5: "The Hallway Remembers" (Callback + Misdirection)
**Format:** Found footage
**Engine:** Misdirection + Callback
**The funny thing:** The Old UNC walks down the hallway. He passes a door he's passed before (callback to Scene 1). This time, the door has a sign: "NOT TODAY." He keeps walking. Another door: "SERIOUSLY." He keeps walking. A third door: "WE TALKED ABOUT THIS." He stops. Stares at it. The door's handle droops apologetically.

**Joke type:** Callback + Personification (the doors know him, they're tired of him)
**Structure:** 0-3s: walking, first door. 3-6s: second and third doors. 7-9s: stares at door. 10s: handle droops.

---

### Scene 6: "The Anti-Joke" (Misdirection + Anti-Joke)
**Format:** Talk show / Found footage
**Engine:** Misdirection (subverted)
**The funny thing:** The Old UNC sits in a talk show chair. The host (off-camera voice) asks: "So, what's new?" The Old UNC stares. Five seconds of silence. The host: "Anything at all?" More silence. The host: "Okay, well, thanks for coming." The Old UNC stands up and leaves. Text card: "~ WuBu ~. We have no idea either."

**Joke type:** Anti-joke (the setup promises an answer; the punchline is that there is no answer)
**Structure:** 0-2s: talk show setup. 2-7s: silence, host's increasing desperation. 8-9s: Old UNC leaves. 10s: text card.

---

### Scene 7: "The Storage Unit Auction" (Exaggeration + Contrast)
**Format:** Commercial / Infomercial
**Engine:** Contrast + Absurd
**The funny thing:** Fast-talking auctioneer voice: "Unit 47! What's in unit 47?!" The Old UNC stands in front of a storage unit. The door opens. Inside: a galaxy. Stars, nebulae, the works. The Old UNC looks at the auctioneer. The auctioneer looks at the galaxy. The auctioneer: "Do I hear $5?" The Old UNC: "I have $5." The auctioneer: "Sold! To the man who will not be traveling to that galaxy." The Old UNC nods.

**Joke type:** Exaggeration (a storage unit contains a galaxy) + Contrast (the auctioneer's enthusiasm vs. the impossibility)
**Structure:** 0-3s: auction setup. 3-6s: galaxy revealed. 6-9s: bidding. 10s: text punchline.

---

### Scene 8: "The Teal Orb's Question" (Absurd + Wordplay)
**Format:** Found footage
**Engine:** Absurd
**The funny thing:** A teal orb floats in front of the Old UNC. It pulses. The Old UNC stares at it. The orb pulses faster. The Old UNC: "I don't speak orb." The orb stops pulsing. It slowly rotates to reveal a tiny screen on its surface. The screen says: "OK." The Old UNC: "Fair enough." They stand there together. Cut.

**Joke type:** Absurdity + Wordplay ("I don't speak orb" → orb adapts)
**Structure:** 0-3s: orb pulsing. 3-5s: Old UNC's line. 5-8s: orb reveals screen. 8-10s: mutual acceptance.

---

### Scene 9: "The Misunderstanding" (Misdirection + Contrast)
**Format:** Sitcom
**Engine:** Misdirection
**The funny thing:** The Old UNC is sitting on a couch. A second character (short, energetic) runs in holding a phone: "It's for you!" The Old UNC takes the phone. Puts it to his ear. Silence. He hands it back. The energetic character: "Well?!" The Old UNC: "It was a picture of a chicken." The energetic character, devastated: "He forgot again." The Old UNC, to camera: "Every time."

**Joke type:** Misdirection (expected: a phone call; got: a photo) + Callback (the chicken)
**Structure:** 0-3s: phone handoff. 3-5s: Old UNC listens. 5-7s: reveals it's a photo. 7-9s: character's devastation. 10s: Old UNC to camera.

---

### Scene 10: "The Button" (Rule of Three + Absurd)
**Format:** Found footage
**Engine:** Absurd + Rule of Three
**The funny thing:** The Old UNC stands in front of a wall with three buttons. He presses button 1. A voice: "Are you sure?" He presses button 2. Voice: "Really sure?" He presses button 3. Voice: "Last chance." He steps back. Looks at the buttons. Presses all three at once. The wall opens to reveal... another wall with three buttons. The Old UNC sits down in front of the new buttons. Text: "He has time."

**Joke type:** Rule of three (three buttons, three warnings) + Absurdity (infinite regression of buttons)
**Structure:** 0-3s: three buttons, first two pressed. 3-6s: third button, warnings. 7-9s: all three, new wall. 10s: text card.

## How to Use This List

These ten scenes are templates. Each one demonstrates a different combination of:
- **Format** (found footage, game show, sitcom, talk show, commercial)
- **Engine** (misdirection, contrast, absurd, normal→absurd)
- **Joke type** (misdirection, absurdity, act-out, exaggeration, wordplay, callback, anti-joke)

When writing a new bumper, pick the scene that's closest to what you want, then swap in your own content. The structure is reusable; the specifics are interchangeable.

## Session Context: The Television Research Behind These Scenes

Each scene is built from specific findings in the television research:

- **Scene 1 (The Door That Isn't)** → Richard Matheson's "A World of Difference" (Lilypad 04, chain-television): An actor on a TV set realizes he's on a real alien planet. The door that isn't a door is the same twist: the thing you thought was normal is actually impossible.

- **Scene 2 (The Game Show That Knows)** → Game show host personas (Lilypad 15, chain-television): The "lost host" concept. Alex Trebek knew all the answers. The Old UNC doesn't even know the questions. The scoreboard showing "∞ to 0" is the Squid Game economic critique (Lilypad 18) applied to existential dread.

- **Scene 3 (The Vending Machine's Opinion)** → Charles Beaumont's "The Howling Man" (Lilypad 03): The Devil is imprisoned and speaks. The vending machine is the imprisoned entity that speaks. The Old UNC's compliance ("He always takes the chicken") is the deadpan reaction Beaumont's characters never gave.

- **Scene 4 (The Height Opposition)** → Matheson's "The Invaders" (Lilypad 04): The perspective twist (the giant is the alien). The height opposition uses the same visual mechanism: the camera makes the tall character "shorter" via perspective.

- **Scene 5 (The Hallway Remembers)** → Doctor Who's "character as host" (Lilypad 20, chain-television): The TARDIS is alive and knows the Doctor. The hallway is alive and knows the Old UNC. The callback structure ("NOT TODAY," "SERIOUSLY") is the memetic television callback mechanism (Lilypad 19).

- **Scene 6 (The Anti-Joke)** → Roddenberry's humanist philosophy (Lilypad 01): The expectation of meaning in a meaningless universe. The anti-joke says: there is no meaning. The text card "~ WuBu ~. We have no idea either." is Roddenberry's optimism inverted.

- **Scene 7 (The Storage Unit Auction)** → Tales from the Crypt's tone (Lilypad 11): Horror + comedy in a commercial format. The auctioneer's enthusiasm + the galaxy inside = the Crypt Keeper's puns + genuine horror.

- **Scene 8 (The Teal Orb's Question)** → The Control Voice from Outer Limits (Lilypad 05, chain-television): "There is nothing wrong with your television set." The orb is the Control Voice — it communicates, it has authority, it's not human. The Old UNC's "I don't speak orb" is the comedy of the human facing the machine.

- **Scene 9 (The Misunderstanding)** → Beaumont's "Person or Persons Unknown" (Lilypad 03): The phone call that isn't a phone call. The misunderstanding is the identity-loss comedy: the caller forgot who the Old UNC is. "Every time" is the callback.

- **Scene 10 (The Button)** → Matheson's "Steel" (Lilypad 04): The robot that dreams of being human. The Old UNC facing infinite buttons is the robot facing infinite fights. "He has time" is the deadpan acceptance.
