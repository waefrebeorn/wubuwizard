## The Complete Toolkit

This lilypad is the master reference — the toolkit the writing room uses to build any 10-second bumper. Everything from lilypads 1-20 is synthesized here into a practical, usable toolkit.

## Part 1: The Seven Joke Types

Every joke in the ~ WuBu ~ universe is one of these seven types (or a combination):

### 1. Misdirection
The audience expects A; they get B. The setup creates a wrong assumption; the punchline flips it.
- **When to use:** When you want a quick, sharp laugh. The backbone of most bumpers.
- **Example:** Old UNC opens a door. Behind it: another door. He nods.

### 2. Analogy
Two unlike things are compared, revealing something true.
- **When to use:** When you want a slower, smarter laugh. Good for character moments.
- **Example:** "The hallway is like a waiting room for a doctor who doesn't exist."

### 3. Exaggeration
A normal situation pushed to an impossible extreme.
- **When to use:** When you want a big, physical laugh. Good for escalation.
- **Example:** The elevator wait time display shows "∞".

### 4. Absurdity
Something that doesn't make logical sense but makes emotional sense.
- **When to use:** When you want the "wait, what... okay that's funny" laugh.
- **Example:** The vending machine says, "That button hasn't worked since the 70s." Old UNC: "I know."

### 5. Wordplay
Humor in the language itself — double meanings, puns, unexpected word choices.
- **When to use:** Text cards, lower-third gags, quick hits.
- **Example:** "WHO WANTS TO BE A MILLIONAIRE'S LAWYER"

### 6. Observation
Pointing out something the audience knew but never articulated.
- **When to use:** Character comedy, world-building, relatable humor.
- **Example:** "Vending machines only eat your money when you're hungry."

### 7. Act-Out
The comedian physically demonstrates something; the demonstration is the joke.
- **When to use:** Visual comedy, physical gags, character bits.
- **Example:** Old UNC tries to fit through a doorway that's too small. He walks away.

## Part 2: The Four Scene Engines

A scene engine is a *way* to generate comedy from a situation. It's the "how" to the joke type's "what."

### Engine 1: Misdirection Engine
Establish a direction → deepen the wrong assumption → flip it.
- **Best joke types:** Misdirection, Wordplay
- **Best formats:** Found footage, horror, talk show
- **10-second shape:** 0-3s establish direction, 3-7s deepen, 7-10s flip

### Engine 2: Contrast Engine
Put two incompatible things in the same space → let them collide.
- **Best joke types:** Exaggeration, Observation, Act-Out
- **Best formats:** Game show, sitcom, commercial
- **10-second shape:** 0-3s establish one side, 3-7s introduce opposite, 7-10s collision

### Engine 3: Absurd Engine
Normal world → absurd event → serious reaction.
- **Best joke types:** Absurdity, Exaggeration
- **Best formats:** Found footage, infomercial, nature documentary
- **10-second shape:** 0-3s normal, 3-6s absurd event, 6-10s deadpan reaction

### Engine 4: "Normal World → Absurd Event" Engine
The simplest engine: show normal, then show not-normal. The gap is the joke.
- **Best joke types:** All seven (this is the universal engine)
- **Best formats:** All formats
- **10-second shape:** 0-4s normal, 4-10s absurd (with escalation inside)

## Part 3: The Structure Rules

These rules apply to every bumper, regardless of joke type or scene engine:

1. **But/Therefore:** Every beat connects causally. No "and then."
2. **One game:** Each bumper has ONE funny thing. Don't cram.
3. **Rule of three:** Establish, reinforce, subvert.
4. **Punch word at the end:** The last word/text/frame is the button.
5. **No jokes in the setup:** The setup is straight. The punchline does the work.
6. **Benign violation:** The twist must be surprising but not threatening.
7. **Three threads max:** Primary action + background gag + text layer. No more.

## Part 5: The Writing Room Workflow

When building a bumper:

1. **Pick a format** (from the 9 channels above)
2. **Pick an engine** (from the 4 scene engines)
3. **Pick a joke type** (from the 7 types)
4. **Apply the structure rules** (but/therefore, one game, rule of three, punch at end)
5. **Run the joke test** (lilypad 23: does it have a real joke?)
6. **Stack if possible** (add a tag, a callback, a second thread)

## Pitfalls
- **Mixing too many joke types** — one primary type per bumper. A second type can appear in the tag.
- **Engine doesn't match format** — the absurd engine in a game show format creates a specific kind of comedy (absurdist game show). If you want contrast comedy, use the contrast engine.
- **Forgetting the audience** — the joke must land in 10 seconds for someone who's never seen ~ WuBu ~ before. Inside jokes are bonuses, not the main event.
