It was written as a Public Access premise and it has become a **Public Access

## WHY THIS ONE IS THE HUNDREDTH AND NOT THE FIRST

It is the only episode in the hundred that **borrows one channel's discipline
inside another channel's rig.** Public Access has the couch, the laugh track and
the height gag; the Archive has unedited found footage and no voice at all. The
joke is that they are watching something — and there is nothing to watch, and no
tape, and no screen, and no voice.

**A tape neither of them recorded has no contents, and the honest staging of that
is an empty box.** So this prompt bars the tape, the cassette, the reel, the
projector, the screen and the monitor by name — because every one of them is a
thing a model will helpfully supply, and each would replace the joke with a
picture.

**The empty box is the props class one final time, and the most exposed instance
in the hundred**, because unlike the tubes, the board and the counter, a box is the
single most fillable object in any scene. I have written it as *one plain flat
empty surface — a clean even inner panel with a visible seam* — a **positive
statement of an interior**, not a description of a hole.

## THE FOUR FLAGS

1. **The box interior is the whole episode.** One clean inner panel with a visible
   seam, identical in every tile. If anything appears inside that box — an object,
   a shadow, a tape, a shape — the episode is dead. This is the strongest
   instruction in the hundred and it is stated in the load-bearing block, the
   silent-frame gamble and the negative block, by name, seven times.

2. **P-8 and P-10 are now a pair** and they must be 6-tiled together. Both are
   two seated figures on the same couch with a plain object between them on the
   carpet. In P-8 the object is a cup and someone wins; in P-10 the object is a box
   and nobody claims it. **If the box arrives at the wrong height or the wrong
   carpet position in P-10, the pair reads as a reshoot** — hold the two boxes
   against each other.

3. **There is no television anywhere in this episode, and that is deliberate.** A
   community-access show about watching something, staged with nothing to watch and
   no screen to watch it on, is the entire joke. Any monitor in frame, even
   powered down, converts it into a normal show. This is the one prompt in the
   hundred where the correct staging is the *absence of a set piece*, and it is
   barfed by name for that reason.

4. **The laugh track is at 21s only** — once, after the box is set down. If the
   track fires on the opening, it laughs at the setup rather than the emptiness,
   and the timing tells the audience which beat is the joke. Check the audio
   positions: silence 0s–21s, one burst, silence to 30s.

## THE THING WORTH REMEMBERING FROM THIS RUN

Nine times the pre-send gate caught the same failure, and it is the most useful
thing the workflow produced:

> **A prop the gag depends on being empty is the prop a generator will fill.**

Unlit tubes. An unplugged board. A blank counter. A blank lanyard card. An empty
corner. A blank lesson card. A dark wall sign. An empty box.

**Every one was fixed by rewriting the instruction as a positive object that is on
screen — and never by re-asking.** Nine rewrites, zero re-asks, and every one caught
before transmission instead of after a rejected clip.
