# NETWORK CONCEPTS — three options for the human to pick from

Written 2026-09-26 in response to WuBu's brief: *"Name the network + channel when
you reply; I will not invent names unilaterally."*

**I am not naming anything unilaterally either.** The brand belongs to the human.
What follows is three one-paragraph concepts, each with a *structure* rather than
a logo, so the choice costs one word. The human picks; the name comes after.

---

## OPTION A — **THE ORDER, SPINNING**
**Shape:** the game show is the flagship and everything else is its afterglow.

`@WuBuSatire` runs NOBODY WON as the network's anchor slot. The other channels
are treated as **segments that exist inside the same broadcast**: the News desk
is where they send reporters to cover the show, Puppet is the variety filler,
Public Access is the late-night call-in that nobody is sure belongs to the
network. The network's whole identity is "one show that keeps being covered by
channels that will not admit they cover it."

- **Strength:** the most self-referential option. Every channel justifies itself
  by pointing at the flagship, so nothing in the slate is filler.
- **Cost:** it makes the satire load-bearing. If NOBODY WON's hold slips, the
  network loses its spine. It also puts all the eggs in the deadpan basket.
- **Fits the canon:** the continuity web already says UNC appears in every
  channel and never acknowledges the others. This is the structure that makes
  that legible.

## OPTION B — **A FOUNDATION**
**Shape:** a public-access network that made itself out of found rooms.

`@WuBuOriginals` is the flagship — the MEGA, the found room, the diegetic
day-stamps. The satire show is **a show the network broadcasts**, News is where
it explains itself, and Labs/MediaSchool is the network openly admitting it is a
production company. The conceit: *the room was real, the crowd was generated, and
this is the paperwork.*

- **Strength:** the most honest option, and it matches the brand thesis
  literally — premise 32, the whole creator-honesty line. It makes the network's
  own production process the subject of its News channel, which is a joke that
  never has to end.
- **Cost:** least funny on its face. It needs the satire to supply the laughs or
  it reads as a documentary about a warehouse.
- **Fits the canon:** exactly what N-3 already does.

## OPTION C — **NINE CHANNELS, NO NETWORK**
**Shape:** refuse the network concept; ship a cable package instead.

Nine channels, equal weight, no flagship, no umbrella brand. Uploaded as one
faceless package, like a public-access lineup that nobody branded. The *absence*
of a network identity is the joke: it is a network that has not noticed it is a
network.

- **Strength:** the most honest to the material and the cheapest to execute. No
  reblocking, no renaming, no inventing. It is also the most robust: nothing can
  be inconsistent with a brand that does not exist.
- **Cost:** harder to sell as a thing. A viewer cannot hold "the network" in
  their head, so it needs the watermark and the ident to do the unifying.
- **Fits the canon:** premise 74 — the network is nine channels that agree about
  one number. The agreement is the only thing holding it together, and nobody
  on camera has noticed.

## THE ONE DECISION THE HUMAN OWNS
Not "which option" — that is mine to recommend, and I recommend **C** for the
launch with **A** as the stated intention, because C ships tonight and A is what
the network grows into once there are enough episodes to earn a flagship.

**But the name is the human's, and the name is the thing worth asking for.**
Three shapes of name, if it helps:
- **Institutional** — sounds like a body that owns buildings (`NETWORK` / `GRID`)
- **Broadcasting** — sounds like a schedule (`CHANNEL` / `THE ... HOUR`)
- **Object** — sounds like a found thing (`THE MEGA` / `UNIT ...`)
