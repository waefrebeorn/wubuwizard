# Lilypad 8/25 — The Sight Gag Toolkit: Humor Without Words
# Domain: Visual Comedy | Chain: writing-room-25 | Depth: Leg 1
# 7-step Kevin Bacon: sight gag → visual humor → physical comedy → silent comedy → plate mechanics

## What Is a Sight Gag?

A sight gag is a joke you see, not hear. No words, no dialogue, no text. Just a visual that contains incongruity. The audience laughs with their eyes.

Sight gags are essential for ~ WuBu ~ because:
1. The Old UNC is a visual character — his stare, his stillness, his presence
2. Found-footage format is inherently visual
3. 10-second bumpers need to work even on mute (social media autoplay is silent)
4. Visual gags are universal — they don't need translation

## The Toolkit

### 1. The Impossible Object
Something that shouldn't exist in the real world, placed in a real setting. A door that opens to sky. A vending machine that dispenses something alive. A hallway that's longer on the inside.

**Why it works:** The brain's pattern-mismatch detector fires. "That object doesn't belong in that context." The resolution: "This is the ~ WuBu ~ world. Things are different here."

### 2. The Wrong Scale
Something is the wrong size. A tiny Old UNC next to a normal-sized door. A giant teal orb in a small room. A storage unit the size of a house.

**Why it works:** Scale is one of the first things the brain processes. Wrong scale = instant incongruity, no setup needed.

### 3. The Physical Impossibility
Something that breaks physics. The Old UNC walks through a wall. An object falls upward. A door opens before the handle is turned.

**Why it works:** Physics is the deepest layer of the brain's world-model. Breaking it is the most fundamental incongruity.

### 4. The Prop Substitution
A normal object is replaced with something absurd. The Old UNC's staff is a pool noodle. His hoodie is a bathrobe. The game show podium is a card table.

**Why it works:** The audience recognizes the *function* of the object but sees the *wrong form*. The gap between function and form is the joke.

### 5. The Background Gag
The joke is happening in the background while the foreground action is normal. The Old UNC stares at the camera while behind him, something impossible unfolds. He doesn't react. That's the joke.

**Why it works:** The audience discovers the gag themselves. The act of discovery is part of the pleasure. And the character's non-reaction makes it benign.

### 6. The Repeated Visual (Running Sight Gag)
A sight gag that appears in multiple bumpers, each time slightly different. The vending machine appears in bumper after bumper, dispensing increasingly absurd items. The audience starts looking for it.

**Why it works:** Recognition + variation. The audience feels smart for recognizing it, then laughs at the new variation.

### 7. The Triple Threat Visual Gag
A sight gag with three stages: establish, escalate, detonate. 
- Stage 1: The Old UNC holds a normal cup.
- Stage 2: He takes a sip. The cup is full again.
- Stage 3: He looks inside the cup. It's infinite. Stars are visible.

**Why it works:** It's the rule of three in visual form. The audience learns the pattern (the cup refills), then the pattern breaks into absurdity (the cup is a portal).

## Silent Comedy Mechanics

The masters of sight gags (Chaplin, Keaton, Tati, Mr. Bean) worked with these principles:
- **The straight man is the world.** The character reacts normally to abnormal situations. The comedy is in the gap between the character's calm and the audience's shock.
- **Escalation is physical.** Each gag is bigger than the last. Start small (a stuck door), build to medium (the door leads to a cliff), end huge (the entire building is a door).
- **The take is everything.** A "take" is a character's reaction to something. The Old UNC's flat stare IS his take. It's the opposite of what the audience expects (panic, surprise), which makes it funnier.

## The 10-Second Application

A sight gag bumper needs:
- **0:00–0:03** — Establish the visual normal (Old UNC in a normal setting)
- **0:03–0:07** — Introduce the impossible element (something wrong appears)
- **0:07–0:10** — The character's reaction (or non-reaction) is the button

No text needed. No dialogue. Just the visual and the timing.

## Pitfalls
- **Too subtle** — if the audience has to pause and look, the gag is too subtle. It should be immediately visible.
- **Too busy** — if there's too much happening visually, the sight gag gets lost. One gag per bumper.
- **Explaining it** — if a character points at the impossible thing and says "Look, that's impossible!" you've killed the gag. Let the audience see it.
