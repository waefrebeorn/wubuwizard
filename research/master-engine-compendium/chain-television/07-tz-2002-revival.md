## Core Facts

- **The Twilight Zone (2002-2003, UPN, 2 seasons, 20 episodes).** A revival that was "doomed from the start" per Slashfilm. The series was produced by **Forest Whitaker** (who also narrated/hosted). Whitaker's narration style was more intimate than Serling's — he spoke directly to the audience as a friend.

- **Forest Whitaker** as host/narrator was the series' most distinctive choice. Whitaker's style: quiet, intense, personal. He would often address the audience directly, with a tone that was more "friend telling a story" than "host presenting a show." This was a deliberate departure from Serling's more theatrical style.

- **Joe Dante directed "It's a Good Life"** — the segment based on the Richard Matheson TZ episode (S3E8, "It's a Good Life," about a boy with godlike powers who terrorizes a town). Dante's version was set in a suburban neighborhood and starred Jeremy Licht as the boy (Gray). Dante's direction was faithful to the original's tone but updated the setting.

- **Other directors:** John Milius (directed an episode), Martha Coolidge (directed an episode), Bill Duke (directed an episode), Atom Egoyan (directed an episode). The director lineup was a mix of veteran genre directors and indie filmmakers. Egoyan's episode was the most stylistically distinct — it had his characteristic slow pacing and psychological tension.

- **Episodes:** 20 episodes across 2 seasons. The series was cancelled after 2 seasons due to low ratings and UPN's declining fortunes (UPN was eventually merged with The WB to form The CW in 2006).

- **Reception:** The series was generally well-received by critics but failed to attract a large audience. The "doomed from the start" narrative comes from the fact that UPN was already in decline, and the series was competing against stronger shows on other networks.
