## What Is "Memetic Television"?

Memetic television is a show whose elements — catchphrases, visual motifs, characters, story beats — spread beyond the audience into popular culture. The show becomes a "meme" — its elements are quoted, shared, remixed, and referenced by people who may not have seen the show. Memetic television is the opposite of "niche" television — it's television that becomes part of the cultural conversation.

## The Mechanisms of Memetic Spread

1. **Catchphrases** — short, repeatable, quotable phrases that capture the show's tone. "Exterminate!" "Don't blink!" "Are you my mummy?" "D'oh!" "That's what she said." These are designed to be repeated — they're the "audio hooks" of memetic television.

2. **Visual motifs** — simple, recognizable visual elements that can be shared as images or GIFs. The Dalek shape, the Weeping Angel statue, the TARDIS blue box, the Crypt Keeper's face, Homer's "D'oh!" gesture. These are designed to be seen — they're the "visual hooks" of memetic television.

3. **Characters with strong personalities** — characters that are easy to imitate, quote, and embody. The Daleks, the Doctor, the Crypt Keeper, Homer Simpson, Cartman, Michael Scott. These are designed to be performed — they're the "character hooks" of memetic television.

4. **Twist endings** — story beats that reveal a new perspective, that subvert expectations. The Twilight Zone's twist endings, Tales from the Crypt's twist endings, Doctor Who's plot twists. These are designed to be discussed — they're the "narrative hooks" of memetic television.

5. **Audience participation** — elements that invite the audience to engage, to quote, to share. The Gong Show's gong (the audience can imagine themselves gonging a performer), Family Feud's answers (the audience can guess the survey answers), Jeopardy!'s questions (the audience can play along at home). These are designed to be interactive — they're the "participation hooks" of memetic television.

6. **Irony and self-awareness** — shows that acknowledge their own format, that play with their own tropes. The Simpsons' self-referential gags, South Park's meta-commentary, Rick and Morty's nihilism. These are designed to be quoted — they're the "irony hooks" of memetic television.
