## The Inversion Principle

Visual inversion comedy is the art of taking a familiar visual grammar and inverting ONE element. The audience recognizes the format immediately. The inversion makes them laugh.

The classic example: **one tall person among short people in a normal setting.** The setting is normal. The situation is normal. The only thing wrong is the height difference. That single inversion is the entire joke.

## Why Inversion Works

1. **Recognition + subversion** — The audience recognizes the format (sitcom, game show, news) and then sees the inversion. The gap between expectation and reality is the laugh.
2. **Minimal change, maximum effect** — You only need to invert ONE element. Change everything and it's chaos. Change one thing and it's comedy.
3. **The audience does the work** — The audience sees the inversion and their brain fills in the implications. "If everyone in this sitcom is short, does that mean the tall person is the oddity? Is this a world where short is normal?" The audience asks these questions themselves. That act of questioning is pleasurable.

## Types of Visual Inversion

### Height Inversion (WuBu Signature)
- One tall person among short people (or vice versa)
- The height difference is treated as normal by the characters
- The comedy comes from the audience seeing what the characters don't

### Scale Inversion
- One giant object among normal objects
- One tiny object among normal objects
- The wrong scale is treated as normal

### Energy Inversion
- One deadpan character in an enthusiastic format
- One enthusiastic character in a deadpan format
- The energy mismatch is the joke

### Format Inversion
- Horror content in a sitcom format
- Game show content in a news format
- The format collision is the joke

### Temporal Inversion
- Modern characters in a period setting
- Period characters in a modern setting
- The anachronism is treated as normal

## Pitfalls
- **Too many inversions** — One inversion per bumper. If you invert height AND scale AND energy, the audience can't track the joke.
- **The inversion isn't visual** — "One character is sad" is not a visual inversion. The inversion must be VISIBLE. Height, scale, color, movement — these are visual.
- **Explaining the inversion** — Don't have a character say "Why is everyone short?" The audience sees it. That's enough.
