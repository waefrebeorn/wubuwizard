## The Descriptor Toolkit

To describe visual grammar without naming brands, you need a vocabulary of generic aesthetic descriptors. This toolkit is organized by visual element:

### Color Descriptors
- **Warm**: amber, gold, cream, wood tones, soft yellow, warm skin tones
- **Cool**: blue, teal, steel gray, fluorescent white, clinical
- **High contrast**: black and white, stark, graphic, bold
- **Muted**: desaturated, faded, pastel, washed-out, analog
- **Vibrant**: saturated, colorful, bright, primary colors, neon

### Lighting Descriptors
- **Sitcom**: warm, ambient, studio lighting, soft shadows, three-point lighting
- **Game show**: bright, even, colorful, spotlights, high-key
- **Horror**: dim, flickering, single-source, harsh shadows, underexposed
- **Found footage**: available light, overexposed, harsh flash, uneven
- **Talk show**: spotlight on desk, soft fill, warm key, monitor glow
- **News**: bright, even, professional, blue-white, studio standard

### Framing Descriptors
- **Sitcom**: medium shot, slightly high angle, three-shot, over-shoulder
- **Game show**: wide shot, low angle (for host), crowd shot, split-screen
- **Horror**: tight framing, Dutch angle, POV shot, static wide
- **Found footage**: handheld, shaky, accidental framing, zoom-in
- **Talk shot**: medium close-up, eye-level, two-shot, over-shoulder
- **News**: medium shot, eye-level, direct address, over-shoulder for field

### Camera Movement Descriptors
- **Sitcom**: static, minimal movement, occasional pan, snap zoom for reaction
- **Game show**: dolly in, crane shot, spin (for wheel), push-in for reveal
- **Horror**: slow push, static, handheld shake, whip pan, tracking
- **Found footage**: handheld, operator movement, accidental, running
- **Talk show**: static, slow push-in, cut between speakers
- **News**: static, occasional pan, cut to field footage

### Texture Descriptors
- **Analog**: VHS degradation, scan lines, chroma bleed, tape noise, CRT curvature
- **Digital**: clean, sharp, compressed, artifacting, pixelated
- **Film**: grain, soft focus, light leaks, warm tones
- **Institutional**: cinderblock, linoleum, fluorescent, metal, concrete
- **Domestic**: wood, fabric, wallpaper, carpet, warm materials

### Style Descriptors
- **1990s**: warm, analog, boxy clothing, practical effects, pre-CGI
- **1970s**: earth tones, film grain, wide collars, shag carpet, wood paneling
- **1980s**: neon, synth, shoulder pads, practical effects, VHS
- **Contemporary**: digital, clean, LED, minimal, flat

## Combining Descriptors

The art is in the combination. A single descriptor is weak. A combination of 5-7 descriptors creates a specific, recognizable grammar:

**Example 1: 1990s Sitcom**
"Warm wood-paneled living room, green couch, coffee table with vase of flowers, framed family photo on wall, warm ambient studio lighting, medium shot slightly high angle, 1990s clothing, laugh track energy"

**Example 2: Analog Horror Broadcast**
"Institutional TV broadcast, CRT curvature, VHS scan lines, chroma bleed, emergency broadcast system graphic, harsh fluorescent lighting, static camera, institutional setting"

**Example 3: Game Show**
"Bright studio lighting, host podium, contestant podiums, audience cheering in background, colorful backdrop, wide shot, enthusiastic energy, scoreboard visible"

## Pitfalls
- **Too many descriptors** — 15 descriptors creates a muddy image. 5-7 strong descriptors is the sweet spot.
- **Conflicting descriptors** — "warm sitcom lighting" + "horror shadows" creates a confused image. Pick a grammar and commit.
- **Forgetting the camera** — The camera IS the grammar. "Found footage" means handheld, shaky, accidental. "Sitcom" means static, medium shot, high angle.
