# NODE 28: Early Tim Burton — Camera Effects, German Expressionism, Stop-Motion, "Vincent", "Beetlejuice", "Batman"

## Core concept
Early Tim Burton = the master engine's VISUAL EFFECTS + CAMERA LANGUAGE: German Expressionism, black-and-white contrast, oblique shapes, forced postures, stop-motion, dutch angles, wide-angle lens distortion, practical effects, gothic atmosphere. The engine's visual identity should channel early Burton.

## Key facts

### "Vincent" (1982) — Burton's first experiment in style
- 1982 stop-motion short horror film written, designed and directed by Tim Burton, produced by Rick Heinrichs
- Burton was working as an animator at Disney, suffered the worst depression of his life, couldn't identify with the work
- Promoted to concept artist for The Black Cauldron, but none of his designs were used
- Julie Hickson (executive) + Tom Wilhite (Director of Creative Development) recognized his talent
- Wilhite gave Burton $60,000 to produce "Vincent" (1982)
- The idea came from a poem written by Burton in the style of Dr. Seuss
- Intended as a children's story but filmed with 3D puppets (framed as an animation test for creative freedom)
- Burton wanted to use three-dimensional figures to give the story "as much realism as possible"
- Vincent Malloy: unconventional 7-year-old boy who dreams of being Vincent Price, imagines himself in situations from Poe's stories
- **Vincent Price himself narrated the story** — Burton's childhood idol, reciting Burton's original poem
- Shot in stark black and white with strong expressionist influences
- **Expressionist aesthetics**: contrasts between light and shadow, oblique shapes, forced postures of characters, iconic expressionist staircases
- Disney executives thought the boy died at the end (reciting Poe's "The Raven"), suggested an alternate ending where the father takes him to a baseball game
- Shown for just a few weeks alongside "Tex" before being buried in archives
- Received several awards at festivals — enough to encourage Burton to continue
- "Vincent" set the tone and style of Burton's entire filmography
- Vincent Malloy = reflection of young Tim Burton (wild hair, suburban discomfort, retreat into fantasy)
- "His thoughts, though, aren't only of ghoulish crimes / He likes to paint and read to pass some of the times / While other kids read books like Go, Jane, Go! / Vincent's favourite author is Edgar Allen Poe"

### "Beetlejuice" (1988)
- Production designer: Bo Welch
- Burton's visual style fully formed: gothic, surreal, darkly comic
- Camera movement + practical effects + production design as the visual language

### "Batman" (1989)
- Production designer: Anton Furst (Oscar winner for the film)
- "The lighting, tones, shadows and gothic atmosphere, was perfection. The cinematography was on another level." (Facebook group)
- Burton uses a mix of medium shots (IPL essay)
- Gothic atmosphere + German Expressionism in the production design
- Camera movement is a key Burton signature (Scene360 analysis)

### Burton's visual signatures (from Scene360 + ArtDepartmental)
- **Camera movement**: "There is a lot of camera movement in the films of Tim Burton"
- **German Expressionism**: contrasts between light and shadow, oblique shapes, forced postures
- **Expressionist staircases**: the iconic visual motif
- **Black-and-white**: stark contrast (Vincent, later Ed Wood)
- **Stop-motion**: 3D puppets, claymation, practical effects
- **Gothic atmosphere**: lighting, tones, shadows
- **Wide-angle lens distortion**: dutch angles, exaggerated perspectives
- **Practical effects**: in-camera tricks, forced perspective, miniatures
- **Protagonist design**: dishevelled, dark-eyed, wild hair — the "Burtonian" look (Edward Scissorhands, Victor Van Dort, Sweeney Todd)
- **Normal surroundings vs tortured protagonist**: the protagonist's individuality against the banally normal world

### What Burton learned at Disney
- "He couldn't identify with the work and didn't fit Disney's style" (Aesthetics Filmclub)
- His response: make his OWN style — expressionist, gothic, personal
- The engine should do the same: don't fit the style of existing commentary systems — make its OWN

## What this means for the engine
- The engine's visual identity = early Burton: German Expressionism, black-and-white contrast, oblique shapes
- The engine's camera language = Burton: lots of camera movement, dutch angles, wide-angle distortion
- The engine's aesthetic = gothic atmosphere, lighting + tones + shadows, practical effects
- The engine's protagonist design = Burtonian: dishevelled, dark-eyed, wild hair, individuality against banal world
- The engine's "Vincent" = the engine's origin story: a personal project that sets the tone for everything after
- The engine = a Burtonian visual machine: expressionist, gothic, stop-motion spirit, practical effects
