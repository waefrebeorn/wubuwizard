## Core Facts

- **Richard Matheson** (1926-2013). Born in Allendale, New Jersey. Died 2013. One of the most influential horror/science fiction writers of the 20th century.
- **Wrote 14 episodes of The Twilight Zone** — third most after Serling (92) and Beaumont (22).
- **Most famous novel**: I Am Legend (1954) — the last man on Earth after a vampire plague. Basis for The Last Man on Earth (1964, Vincent Price), The Omega Man (1971, Charlton Heston), I Am Legend (2007, Will Smith).
- **Other major novels**: The Shrinking Man (1956, adapted as The Incredible Shrinking Man, 1957), A Stir of Echoes (1958), Hell House (1971, adapted as The Legend of Hell House, 1973).
- **Also wrote for**: The Alfred Hitchcock Hour, The Danny Thomas Show, The Third Man, Burke's Law, and was a prolific short story writer.

## Key Twilight Zone Episodes by Matheson

Season 1 (1959-1960):
- "And When the Sky Was Opened" (S1E11) — based on Matheson's short story "The Test." Three astronauts begin disappearing one by one after a space mission. Written by Matheson (teleplay), based on his story. Directed by Douglas Heyes.
- "The Last Flight" (S1E18) — a WWI pilot from 1918 appears in a modern air traffic control tower, thinking it's still 1918. Written by Matheson. Directed by William F. Claxton.
- "Third from the Sun" (S1E14) — a family escapes an atomic war on Earth by flying to a distant planet, only to discover they've landed on Earth's moon and the war is already here. Based on Matheson's short story. Written by Matheson (teleplay). Directed by Richard L. Bare.
- "A World of Difference" (S1E23) — an actor on a TV set realizes he's actually on a real alien planet and his "show" is a ritual. Written by Matheson. Directed by Ted Post.
- "A World of His Own" (S1E36) — a playwright creates characters that become real, then tries to erase them. Written by Matheson. Directed by Ralph Nelson.

Season 2 (1960-1961):
- "Steel" (S2E15) — a robot boxer is given a chance to fight for its freedom. Written by Matheson. Directed by David Alexander. One of the most famous TZ episodes — the robot's dream of being human.
- "Little Girl Lost" (S2E31) — a girl falls into a fourth dimension through her bedroom floor. Written by Matheson. Directed by David Alexander.

Season 3 (1961-1962):
- "The Hitch-Hiker" (S1E16) — a woman keeps seeing a hitchhiker who is repeatedly killed in accidents, and realizes he is her own death. Based on Lucille Fletcher's radio play. Teleplay by Rod Serling, based on the story. (NOT by Matheson — common misattribution.)
- "The Invaders" (S3E15) — a lonely farmwoman fights off tiny invaders from a spaceship. Written by Matheson. Directed by Douglas Heyes. Famous for the twist: the "invaders" are actually alien beings, and the farmwoman is a giant.
- "The Mirror" is Beaumont, not Matheson. (Common confusion.)

## WuBu Relevance

Matheson's TZ episodes share a specific quality: ordinary people confronted with extraordinary circumstances, usually with a twist that reveals something about THEM. "Steel" — a robot that wants to be human. "The Invaders" — a tiny woman fighting giants. "Little Girl Lost" — a child lost in another dimension. This is the WuBuMovie's core: the Old UNC walking through storage units, and the twist is always that the storage unit is a portal, or the Order is real, or the character is already dead. Matheson's technique: write the ordinary world with absolute specificity, then break it with ONE extraordinary element. Not a flood of weirdness. One crack in the ordinary.
