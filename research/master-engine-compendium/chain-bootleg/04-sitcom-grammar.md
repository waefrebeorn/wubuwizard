## The Sitcom Visual Grammar

The situation comedy has a specific visual grammar that audiences recognize instantly. You don't need to name a single show — you describe the grammar, and the audience's brain fills in the rest.

### The Core Elements

1. **Multi-camera setup**: 3-4 cameras shooting simultaneously, usually from a high angle slightly above eye level
2. **Warm lighting**: Three-point lighting, soft shadows, warm color temperature (amber/gold)
3. **Three-wall set**: The room has three visible walls (the fourth is where the camera is), often with a staircase visible
4. **Laugh track**: Studio audience laughter, timed to punchlines, warm and supportive
5. **Medium shots**: Most coverage is medium shots and medium close-ups, with occasional wide shots for physical comedy
6. **Snap zoom**: Quick zoom into a character's face for reaction shots
7. **Warm color palette**: Wood tones, cream, green, warm skin tones, soft fabrics

### The Set Design Grammar

**Living Room Set:**
- Couch (usually green, burgundy, or earth tone)
- Coffee table (with vase of flowers, magazines, remote controls)
- Framed family photos on the wall
- Lamp in the corner
- Kitchen visible through a doorway
- Stairs visible (leading to bedrooms)
- Warm wood paneling or wallpaper

**Kitchen Set:**
- Kitchen island or dining table
- Refrigerator with magnets/family photos
- Cabinets (usually wood-tones)
- Warm overhead lighting
- Window over the sink

### The Comedy Grammar

Sitcom comedy has its own visual rules:
- **Reaction shot**: Cut to a character's face after a punchline, hold for the laugh
- **Walk and talk**: Characters move through the set while delivering lines
- **Physical comedy**: Tripping, falling, spilling — shot in wide to see the full body
- **The "look"**: A character looks at the camera (or just past it) to share a moment with the audience
- **Cross-cutting**: Two conversations happening simultaneously, cut between them

### The 1990s Sitcom Grammar (Specific)

For the ~ WuBu ~ height opposition bumpers, the 1990s sitcom grammar is the target:
- Boxy clothing (oversized jeans, button-down shirts, blouses)
- Warm, slightly overexposed lighting
- Practical sets (real locations, not green screen)
- Film grain (shot on film, not digital)
- Laugh track (not just applause — actual laughter)
- Title card with cast names over a freeze frame

## Pitfalls
- **Forgetting the camera** — Sitcom grammar IS camera grammar. "Multi-cam, medium shot, high angle" is as important as the set description.
- **Wrong era** — 1970s sitcoms (shag carpet, earth tones) look different from 1990s (wood paneling, warm tones). Specify the era.
- **Missing the laugh track** — The laugh track is the sitcom's audio grammar. Without it, the audience reads the scene as drama.
