## Core Facts

- **Doctor Who (2005-present, modern series).** Revived by **Russell T Davies** (RTD) for the BBC. The modern series brought Doctor Who back after a 16-year hiatus (1989-2005). RTD's era (2005-2010) was the "family-friendly" era — the Doctor (Christopher Eccleston, then David Tennant) was a more emotional, more "human" Doctor than the classic series. RTD's features: accelerated pacing (the classic series' 4-6 episode serials became 1-2 episode stories with a 45-minute runtime), emotional character arcs (the Doctor's relationship with Rose Tyler was the emotional core of the 2005 series), and a "showrunner as auteur" model (RTD controlled the tone, the stories, the casting).

- **Russell T Davies** (born 1963) — the showrunner of Doctor Who from 2005-2010. Before Who, Davies created "Queer as Folk" (1999-2000), "Bob & Rose" (2002), "The Second Coming" (2003), and "Casanova" (2005). Davies' style: emotional, character-driven, populist. Davies' Doctor Who: the Doctor as a "romantic hero" — the show was about the Doctor's relationships, his loneliness, his need for connection. Davies' best story: "The Day of the Doctor" (2013, special) — the 50th anniversary story that brought back multiple Doctors (Matt Smith, David Tennant, John Hurt as the War Doctor) and resolved the Zygon invasion plot.

- **Steven Moffat** (born 1968) — the showrunner of Doctor Who from 2010-2017. Before Who, Moffat was a television writer and showrunner who created "Coupling" (2000-2002, a British sitcom that was the basis for "Friends") and wrote for "Jekyll" (2007). Moffat's style: psychological, complex, puzzle-box. Moffat's Doctor Who: the Doctor as a "mystery" — the show was about the Doctor's past, his secrets, his darker side. Moffat's best stories: "Blink" (2007, the Weeping Angels), "The Empty Child"/"The Doctor Dances" (2005, the gas mask children), "Silence in the Library"/"Forest of the Dead" (2008, the Library), "The Time of the Doctor" (2013, the Clara/Doctor relationship).

- **Paul Cornell** (born 1967) — wrote "Human Nature" (2007) and "The Family of Blood" (2007), which together form one of the best modern Who stories. Cornell's story: the Doctor becomes human (using a Chameleon Arch) and lives as a schoolteacher named John Smith in 1913 England, falling in love with a nurse named Joan Redfern. The Family of Blood (a group of immortal aliens) hunt the Doctor, and the story ends with the Doctor revealing himself and taking brutal revenge on them. Cornell's style: emotional, literary, character-driven. "Human Nature"/"Family of Blood" is the best modern Who story for its portrayal of the Doctor's humanity — the Doctor as a man, not a god.

- **Mark Gatiss** (born 1966) — wrote "The Snowmen" (2012), "The Bells" (2013), and "The Snowdrop Festival" (wait, that's not a Gatiss story). Gatiss is a writer and showrunner who created "Sherlock" (2010-2017, the BBC's modern Sherlock Holmes adaptation) and "Leninal" (2019). Gatiss' Doctor Who stories: "The Rebel Flesh"/"The Almost People" (2010, the Doctor-Doppelganger story), "The Snowmen" (2012, the Christmas special where the Doctor is depressed and builds a snowman army), "The Bells" (2013, the horror story set in a town where the church bells kill people). Gatiss' style: genre-blending, horror-tinged, revivalist.

- **Best episodes by memetic impact:**

  - **"Blink" (2007, written by Steven Moffat)** — the Weeping Angels. The Angels are quantum-locked statues that move only when you're not looking at them. The story is a masterclass in suspense — the protagonist (Sally Sparrow, played by Carey Mulligan) communicates with the Doctor through video greetings on a DVD player, and the Doctor solves the mystery from a distance. "Blink" is the most memetic Doctor Who story — the Weeping Angels are one of the most recognizable monsters in modern television. The phrase "Don't blink" is a cultural meme.

  - **"The Empty Child"/"The Doctor Dances" (2005, written by Steven Moffat)** — the gas mask children. A little girl with a gas mask (played by Daisy Addison) asks "Are you my mummy?" repeatedly. The Doctor (David Tennant) solves the mystery by dancing with a ghost ship. The story is the best Moffat story for its tone — the horror of the gas mask children, the comedy of the Doctor's dancing. The phrase "Are you my mummy?" is a cultural meme.

  - **"Silence in the Library"/"Forest of the Dead" (2008, written by Steven Moffat)** — the Library. A library at the end of the universe that contains every book that was ever written. The Doctor (David Tennant) and Donna (Catherine Tate) investigate the library, where a creature (the Vash) is killing people. The story is the best Moffat story for its scope — the Library is a "museum of all knowledge" concept. The phrase "Silence in the Library" is a cultural meme.

  - **"The Day of the Doctor" (2013, written by Steven Moffat)** — the 50th anniversary special. Three Doctors (Matt Smith, David Tennant, John Hurt as the War Doctor) come together to solve the Zygon invasion plot. The story is the best modern Who story for its scope — it brings back the classic series' legacy (the War Doctor, played by John Hurt, is a previously unseen incarnation of the Doctor who fought in the Time War). The phrase "The Day of the Doctor" is a cultural meme.
