## Core Facts

- **The "game show" as a television format** emerged in the 1950s. The first game shows were radio formats adapted to television — quiz shows, panel shows, variety shows with audience participation.

- **Jeopardy!** (1964-present, most famously hosted by Alex Trebek from 1984-2020). Created by **Merv Griffin** (who also created "Wheel of Fortune"). The key innovation: the "answer-and-question" format — contestants are given an answer and must respond with the question. This was a reversal of the standard quiz show format (question → answer). The format was inspired by Merv Griffin's wife, Julann Griffin, who suggested it while playing a game with their son. Jeopardy! is the most successful game show in television history — it has been running continuously since 1964 (with a hiatus during the original run's late 1970s-early 1980s syndicated version).

- **The Price Is Right** (1956-1965 with Bill Cullen; 1972-present with Bob Barker; 2007-present with Drew Carey). Created by **Bill Cullen** (original host) and **Mark Goodson** and **Bill Todman** (the production team). The show's format: contestants guess the prices of consumer goods, with the closest guess winning the item. The show is the longest-running game show in American television history (since 1972).

- **Wheel of Fortune** (1975-present, most famously hosted by Pat Sajak from 1981-2024 and Vanna White since 1982). Created by **Merv Griffin**. The format: a word puzzle wheel, contestants spin the wheel and guess letters to solve the puzzle. The show is the second-longest-running game show in American television history (after The Price Is Right).

- **Family Feud** (1976-1985 with Richard Dawson; 1999-2010 with Louie Anderson; 2010-present with Steve Harvey). Created by **Richard Dawson** (original host) and **Mark Goodson** and **Bill Todman**. The format: two families compete to guess the most popular answers to survey questions. The show's signature moment: Richard Dawson kissing the women on the show (a scandalous practice that was a running joke). The show is the most popular syndicated game show in the United States.

- **Match Game** (1963-1969 with Gene Rayburn; 1973-1979 with Gene Rayburn in the "Match Game PM" revival). Created by **Frank Wayne** and **Bob Stewart**. The format: contestants match answers to fill-in-the-blank questions, with the goal of matching the answers of a panel of celebrities. The show's signature: the "fill-in-the-blank" format and the panel of comedians (Gene Rayburn hosted with a panel that included **Charles Nelson Reilly**, **Betty White**, **Phyllis Diller**, etc.). The show was known for its double-entendre humor — the fill-in-the-blank questions often had risqué answers.

- **The Gong Show** (1976-1980 with Gary Collins; 2008 with Nick Swardson; 2014-2015 with Brooke Burns). Created by **Gary Collins** (host) and **Ricou Browning** (producer). The format: amateur performers perform, and the judges (a panel of celebrities) can gong them off stage if the performance is bad. The show's signature: the gong — the moment when the judge hits the gong and the performer is "gonged." The show was a cult hit and a symbol of 1970s television excess.

- **Card Sharks** (1978-1989 with Jim Perry; 1986-1989 with Bob Barker in the daytime version). Created by **Bob Stewart** (who also created "Match Game" and "The Price Is Right"). The format: contestants answer questions about the values of playing cards, with the goal of stealing the opponent's cards. The show's signature: the card values (the face cards are worth more than the number cards), the "stealing" mechanic.

- **The $100,000 Pyramid** (1973-1974 with Dick Clark; 1982-1983 with Dick Clark; various revivals with Donny Osmond). Created by **Bob Stewart**. The format: a word/guessing game where one player gives clues and the other guesses the word. The show's signature: the "pyramid" of words, the "clue-giver" and "guesser" roles.

- **Hollywood Squares** (1966-1982 with Peter Marshall; 1986-1989 with John Davidson). Created by **Don Selzer** (who also created "The Hollywood Squares" radio show). The format: a tic-tac-toe grid of celebrities, contestants answer trivia questions to claim squares, and the celebrities' answers determine whether the square is won. The show's signature: the "Hollywood" celebrities in a tic-tac-toe grid, the "wise" or "foolish" answers from the panel.

## The "Host Persona" in Game Shows

- The game show host is a specific television role: the host is not just a presenter, but a personality who sets the tone of the show. The host's persona is the show's identity. **Alex Trebek** (Jeopardy!) was the "wise, dignified" host — his persona was authority, trustworthiness, and cultural literacy. **Bob Barker** (The Price Is Right) was the "warm, avuncular" host — his persona was friendliness, humor, and the famous "Come on down!" catchphrase. **Pat Sajak** (Wheel of Fortune) was the "witty, cynical" host — his persona was humor, irony, and the famous "I'm Pat Sajak, and this is Wheel of Fortune!" intro. **Richard Dawson** (Family Feud) was the "charming, unpredictable" host — his persona was warmth, unpredictability, and the famous kiss. **Gene Rayburn** (Match Game) was the "goofy, irreverent" host — his persona was humor, double-entendre, and the famous "match the answer" catchphrase. **Gary Collins** (The Gong Show) was the "smooth, sarcastic" host — his persona was wit, sarcasm, and the famous "I'm Gary Collins, and this is The Gong Show!" intro.
