## The Format Cheat Sheet

A quick-reference guide to the visual grammar of every television format the ~ WuBu ~ channels use:

### SITCOM (Multi-Camera)
- **Camera**: Medium shot, slightly high angle, multi-cam setup
- **Lighting**: Warm, ambient, studio three-point
- **Set**: Three-wall set, living room or kitchen, warm colors
- **Audio**: Laugh track, studio audience, ambient studio noise
- **Color**: Warm — wood tones, cream, green, earth tones
- **Energy**: Bright, warm, family-friendly (unless subverted)

### SITCOM (Single-Camera)
- **Camera**: Medium shot, eye-level, cinematic framing
- **Lighting**: Naturalistic, available light, less warm than multi-cam
- **Set**: Real locations, not studio sets
- **Audio**: No laugh track, ambient sound, music
- **Color**: Desaturated, cooler, more realistic
- **Energy**: Quieter, more observational, drier

### GAME SHOW
- **Camera**: Wide shot, low angle for host, crowd shots
- **Lighting**: Bright, high-key, colorful, no shadows
- **Set**: Host podium, contestant podiums, audience seating, colorful backdrop
- **Audio**: Buzzer sounds, audience cheering, enthusiastic music
- **Color**: Bright primary colors — blue, red, yellow
- **Energy**: Enthusiastic, loud, competitive

### TALK SHOW
- **Camera**: Medium close-up, eye-level, two-shot
- **Lighting**: Spotlight on desk, soft fill, warm key
- **Set**: Desk, guest chair, monitor, band (for late-night)
- **Audio**: Conversational, band music (for late-night), audience laughter
- **Color**: Warm, professional, blue backdrop (for late-night)
- **Energy**: Conversational, intimate, witty

### NEWS
- **Camera**: Medium shot, eye-level, direct address
- **Lighting**: Bright, even, professional, blue-white
- **Set**: News desk, monitors, studio backdrop with city skyline or graphics
- **Audio**: Serious, authoritative, no laugh track
- **Color**: Cool — blue, gray, white, professional
- **Energy**: Serious, authoritative, urgent

### HORROR (Analog/Found Footage)
- **Camera**: Handheld, shaky, accidental framing, static wide shots
- **Lighting**: Available light, flickering, underexposed, fluorescent
- **Set**: Liminal spaces, hallways, storage units, institutional settings
- **Audio**: Ambient hum, tape hiss, silence, institutional sounds
- **Color**: Desaturated, green tint (night vision), CRT curvature, VHS degradation
- **Energy**: Uncanny, unsettling, slow dread

### INFOMERCIAL
- **Camera**: Medium shot, direct address, split-screen for before/after
- **Lighting**: Bright, even, slightly overexposed
- **Set**: Living room set or studio set, product display
- **Audio**: Enthusiastic voice-over, "But wait, there's more!", call to action
- **Color**: Bright, saturated, product-focused
- **Energy**: Enthusiastic, sales-oriented, urgent

### COMMERCIAL
- **Camera**: Varied — medium shots, close-ups, lifestyle shots
- **Lighting**: Bright, polished, aspirational
- **Set**: Lifestyle settings — home, car, workplace, outdoors
- **Audio**: Music, voice-over, tagline, jingle
- **Color**: Bright, saturated, brand-consistent
- **Energy**: Aspirational, emotional, quick cuts

### SPORTS BROADCAST
- **Camera**: Wide shot, low angle, crowd shots, replay
- **Lighting**: Stadium lighting, bright, even
- **Set**: Arena, field, court, stadium
- **Audio**: Announcer play-by-play, crowd noise, music
- **Color**: Team colors, green field, bright uniforms
- **Energy**: Exciting, competitive, loud

## Session Context

This cheat sheet was built from the format research in session `20260817_171820_a94bc1`. Each format's grammar was derived from analyzing the visual conventions of dozens of shows in that format — without naming any specific show. The grammar is the common denominator across all shows in the format.

## Pitfalls
- **Mixing format grammars** — Don't use sitcom lighting with horror camera work unless you're deliberately creating a format collision.
- **Forgetting the audio** — Audio is half the format grammar. Sitcom = laugh track. Game show = buzzer. Horror = ambient hum.
- **Wrong era** — Specify the era. 1990s sitcom grammar ≠ 2020s sitcom grammar.
