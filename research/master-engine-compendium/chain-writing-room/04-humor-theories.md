# Lilypad 4/25 — The Theories of Humor: Why Things Are Funny (The Full Stack)
# Domain: Comedy Theory | Chain: writing-room-25 | Depth: Leg 1
# 7-step Kevin Bacon: humor theories → incongruity → superiority → relief → misattribution → O'Shannon

## Why Study Theory?

Because when a joke doesn't work, theory tells you *why*. Each theory is a diagnostic tool. If your joke isn't landing, check which theory you're relying on and whether you've executed it. Theory is the debugging toolkit for comedy.

## Theory 1: Incongruity Theory (the dominant model)

**Kant:** "Laughter is an affection arising from the sudden transformation of a strained expectation into nothing."

**Kierkegaard:** The comic is wherever there is contradiction, and wherever the contradiction is painless.

**How it works:** The brain builds a model of how the world works. A joke presents something that doesn't fit the model (incongruity). The brain scrambles to resolve it. If it can resolve it in a surprising way — laugh. If it can't resolve it — confusion. If the incongruity is threatening — fear, not laughter.

**The key insight:** The laugh IS the resolution. The audience doesn't laugh *after* understanding the joke — the understanding and the laugh are the same neurological event.

**Application:** Your punchline must be surprising but *make sense in retrospect*. If it's just random, the audience can't resolve the incongruity. If it's predictable, there's no incongruity to resolve. The sweet spot is "I didn't see it coming, but it's the only thing that could have happened."

## Theory 2: Superiority Theory (Hobbes, Plato)

**Hobbes:** "Sudden glory arising from sudden conception of some eminency in ourselves, by comparison with the infirmity of others."

**How it works:** We laugh at other people's misfortunes, mistakes, or inadequacies because it makes us feel superior. Slapstick is the purest form — someone slips on a banana peel, and we laugh because we didn't.

**The benign violation version:** We laugh when someone is "brought down" but not actually hurt. The key word is *benign* — if the misfortune is real and serious, we don't laugh. If it's trivial or the person is "asking for it," we do.

**Application:** The Old UNC is a perfect superiority-theory vehicle. He's dignified, serious, philosophical — and then something undignified happens to him. The audience laughs because they're not him, and because the fall is benign. He's not hurt. He's just... slightly embarrassed. That gap between dignity and embarrassment is comedy gold.

## Theory 3: Relief Theory (Freud, Spencer)

**Freud:** Jokes release psychic energy that was being used to repress taboo thoughts. The laugh is the release of that energy.

**Spencer:** Laughter is an economical phenomenon — an overflow of nervous energy that occurs when expectation is built up and then deflated.

**How it works:** Tension builds. The punchline releases it. The energy that was being used to maintain the tension has to go somewhere — it comes out as laughter.

**Application:** This is why pacing matters. If you rush the punchline, the tension hasn't built enough — weak laugh. If you drag the setup too long, the tension dissipates — weak laugh. The punchline must come at the exact moment of maximum tension. In a 10-second bumper, that's around second 7-8.

## Theory 4: Misattribution Theory (Zillmann)

**How it works:** We laugh at people we perceive as "other" — different political views, different social groups, different behaviors. The laughter is a form of social distancing. "I'm not like that person."

**The dark side:** This is the theory behind bigoted humor. It's also the theory behind why self-deprecating humor works (the comedian takes the hit before the audience can).

**Application:** Use carefully. The ~ WuBu ~ world uses misattribution comedy when it makes a character the "other" for comedic purposes — but the target must be fair game (powerful, absurd, or self-implied), not a vulnerable group.

## Theory 5: O'Shannon Model (the synthesis)

**O'Shannon (2012):** Humor requires four simultaneous conditions:
1. **Context** — the situation is framed as non-threatening
2. **Contrast** — the perception contains incongruity
3. **Disposition** — the perceiver has a stance toward the contrast (it's benign)
4. **Sense of humor** — the perceiver is in a playful state

**Why this matters:** If any one of these is missing, the joke fails. Great joke + wrong context = no laugh. Great joke + hostile audience = no laugh. This is why the same joke kills at one show and dies at another.

## The WuBu Writing Room Stack

For 10-second bumpers, the primary theories are:
1. **Incongruity** (the main engine — surprise + resolution)
2. **Superiority** (the Old UNC's dignity vs. indignity)
3. **Relief** (tension built in 7 seconds, released in the last 3)

Use the others situationally. But always check: does my joke create incongruity that resolves? Does it build tension and release it? Does it let the audience feel safely superior?
