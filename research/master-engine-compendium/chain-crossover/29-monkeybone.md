# NODE 29: Monkeybone — Henry Selick, Stop-Motion/Live-Action Hybrid, Surreal Nightmare, Cult Failure

## Core concept
Monkeybone (2001) = the master engine's HYBRID FORMAT model: stop-motion animation + live-action, surreal nightmare logic, dark fantasy comedy, a cult failure that's MORE interesting than a success. The engine can use this as its "format hybrid + cult aesthetic" mode.

## Key facts

### The film
- Directed by Henry Selick (The Nightmare Before Christmas, James and the Giant Peach, Coraline)
- Written by Sam Hamm (Batman 1989)
- Produced by Michael Barnathan + Mark Radcliffe; executive produced by Chris Columbus, Selick, Hamm
- Combining live action with mostly stop-motion animation
- Loosely based on Kaja Blackley's graphic novel "Dark Town"
- Stars Brendan Fraser, Bridget Fonda, Chris Kattan, Giancarlo Esposito, Rose McGowan, Whoopi Goldberg, John Turturro (voice of Monkeybone)
- Released February 23, 2001 by 20th Century Fox
- Box-office bomb; received generally negative critical reviews for characters and humor
- Praise for the visuals (Metacritic: 40/100, mixed/average)

### The premise
- Brendan Fraser as Stu Miley: "beloved genius iconoclastic underground cartoonist" and "creator of America's most disturbed comic strip"
- Stu created "Monkeybone" — a cartoon based on his childhood boner incident (tried to cover it with his backpack, a monkey named Monkeybone jumped out and danced around)
- "The Comedy Channel has picked us up with an order of six episodes" — the pilot is a 2-minute cartoon explaining the premise
- Stu's agent Herb (Dave Foley) already manufactured Monkeybone dolls, bop bags, backpacks, slippers, telephones — working on a deal with "Burger God"
- Stu hates the attention + wants to propose to his girlfriend Julie (Bridget Fonda), who is also the sleep institute doctor who cured his nightmares
- A giant Monkeybone balloon inflates in the backseat causing a car accident that puts Stu in a coma
- His spirit sinks into the stretcher → wakes up on a rollercoaster in stop-motion limbo

### "Down Town" — the coma world
- A limbo for souls of people in comas until they die or wake up
- "Sub-BEETLEJUICE type jokes" — suitcases next to a sign that says "psychological baggage"
- The Morpheum theater where they watch people's nightmares
- Coma Bar with bumper cars connected to IVs
- Miss Kitty (Rose McGowan): sexy cat-person waitress
- An elephant plays piano; Monkeybone (stop motion, voiced by John Turturro) dances and dresses as Marilyn Monroe
- "Years before Pixar asked 'What if emotions were people?' Selick asked 'What if a boner was a monkey?'"
- Hypnos, the God of Nightmares (Giancarlo Esposito): a satyr with little puppet goat legs + bee-woman girlfriend
- Medusa singing "Love is the Drug," a Yeti (Doug Jones) dancing, a bug with a human face
- Death (Whoopi Goldberg) + Thomas Haden Church as Death's right hand man
- The Exit Pass: a shroud that lets you wake up from your coma — fired from a highstrike into the glowing mouth of a stone Abe Lincoln
- Stu gets thrown against a Mark Ryden painting → briefly sucked into a 3D live-action/CG re-enactment of it (Brendan Fraser face on a creepy root/larva baby body)
- Monkeybone hits Stu over the head with a wrench and steals the Exit Pass: "Love you too!"

### The Monkeybone character
- "The character of Roger Rabbit is intended to be a pain in the ass to Eddie Valiant but endearing to most of the audience. For Monkeybone they don't even feint at the endearing part, they just make him hyper and aggressively unfunny. But well animated." (Vern)
- Monkeybone = Stu's erection personified: "That's seriously the premise"
- At the Coma Bar: Stu grabs Monkeybone saying "You have humiliated me in the last time" and demands he get into his backpack (reference to covering his boner with a backpack)
- Miss Kitty purrs at him → Stu gets lusty → Monkeybone climbs out: "being repressed"

### Why it's a cult classic
- "There's way more random cool shit in it than in most movies" (Vern)
- "Visually stunning film that blends stop-motion animation and live action" (Facebook)
- "A bizarre joy of a film... clearly inspired at numerous points by Tim Burton's Beetlejuice" (Counter Arts Medium)
- "Dark stop-motion-inspired visuals and irreverent humor in a way that recalls the tone and rhythm of Beetlejuice" (TikTok @drake.by.33)
- "Surreal blend of live action, stop-motion animation, adult humor, grim fantasy" (The Frida Cinema)
- "Monkeybone himself and several other creatures were created with stop-motion technology and it worked very well" (UKFilmNerd)
- "A neat little blend of stop motion spliced with some characters, their mouths or legs being stop motion" (Blu-ray Forum)
- The earthquake story: Vern watched Monkeybone during the Feb 28, 2001 Seattle earthquake — thought the building would collapse

### Henry Selick's legacy
- Primarily known for stop-motion: Coraline, James and the Giant Peach, The Nightmare Before Christmas
- Monkeybone = the weird middle child: too dark for kids, too weird for adults, too unfunny for critics
- But the VISUALS are praised — the stop-motion is excellent
- "The short Disney adventure into stop motion ended" after James and the Giant Peach — Selick's work was too dark/bizarre for Disney
