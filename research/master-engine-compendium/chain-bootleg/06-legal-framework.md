## The Legal Framework

Understanding the legal framework lets you push the boundary of evocation without crossing into infringement. The key concepts:

### Fair Use (US Copyright Law)
Four factors determine fair use:
1. **Purpose and character of the use** — Transformative uses (commentary, criticism, parody) are favored over commercial uses
2. **Nature of the copyrighted work** — Using factual works is more likely to be fair than using creative works
3. **Amount and substantiality** — Using less of the original work is more likely to be fair
4. **Effect on the market** — If your use doesn't compete with the original, it's more likely to be fair

### Parody Protection
Parody is specifically protected under fair use. A parody must:
- Comment on or criticize the original work (not just use it for attention)
- Use only as much of the original as necessary to "conjure up" the reference
- Be transformative (add new meaning, not just copy)

### Trademark Law
Trademark protects brand names, logos, and trade dress (the visual identity of a product). To avoid trademark infringement:
- Don't use brand names in prompts
- Don't describe logos or specific visual trademarks
- Don't create confusion about sponsorship or endorsement

### Right of Publicity
The right of publicity protects a person's likeness from being used without permission. To avoid violations:
- Don't use celebrity names in prompts
- Don't describe specific real people (use generic descriptions)
- Avoid "guest star" or "cameo" language (this also triggers AI moderation)

## Pitfalls
- **Over-caution** — Being so safe that you describe nothing. Grammar is legal. Specificity is legal. Only brand names and trademarks are risky.
- **Confusing copyright with trademark** — Copyright protects creative works (characters, stories). Trademark protects brand identity (names, logos). You can legally describe a "time-traveling eccentric" — you can't legally call them "The Doctor."
- **Forgetting the AI moderation layer** — Even if something is legally fair use, the AI's content moderation may flag it. Avoid celebrity names, cameos, and guest-star language regardless of legal status.
