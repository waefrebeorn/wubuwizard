## Classic Series Slop

- **"The Curse of Fatal Death" (1999, BBC charity special)** — a parody of Doctor Who written by Steven Moffat and Mark Gatiss. The story is intentionally bad — the Doctor (Rowan Atkinson) regenerates rapidly (multiple actors playing the Doctor in quick succession), the plot is a parody of Who tropes, and the whole thing is a comedy sketch. This is not "slop" in the sense of being bad — it's intentionally funny. But it's a warning: parody Who can become the real thing in the audience's mind.

- **"The Infinity Doctor" (unproduced)** — a proposed story by **BBC**? No. There's no "Infinity Doctor" story that's widely known as slop. Let me focus on the known slop.

- **The "slop" of the classic series** is not a specific story — it's the production hell of the late 1980s. The classic series declined in quality in the late 1980s (seasons 24-26, the Seventh Doctor era). The decline was due to: budget cuts, the BBC's lack of interest in the show, the writer burnout (Robert Holmes, the most prolific writer, was exhausted), and the show's increasing self-importance (the Seventh Doctor's "master manipulator" era became too convoluted). The "slop" of classic Who is the late 1980s — seasons 24-26, the stories that are hard to find, the ones that are not remembered.

## Modern Series Slop

- **"The Doctor's Wife" (2011, written by Neil Gaiman)** — wait, this is a GOOD story. Neil Gaiman's "The Doctor's Wife" is a well-regarded story about the TARDIS's consciousness being put into a human body (Idris, played by actress..?). This is not slop.

- **The "slop" of modern Who** is the post-Moffat era (2018-2022) and the RTD return (2023-present). **Chritopher Chibnall's era (2018-2022)** is widely considered the lowest point of modern Who — the "slop" era. Chibnall's features: the "forgiveness" theme (the Doctor learns to forgive his enemies), the "Graham/Val/ Ryan" TARDIS team (the most "ordinary" companions in Who history), and the "Doctor Who: The Edge of Reality" game tie-in (a video game that was a marketing fiasco). Chibnall's best story: "Demons of the Punjab" (2018, the Partition of India story) — a genuinely good story that was overshadowed by the overall decline. Chibnall's worst: "The Time of the Monster" (2022, the finale, widely criticized).

- **The "slop" of the Chibnall era** is not just the stories — it's the production model. Chibnall's Who was shot on a tight budget, with a small writing team (Chibnall wrote many episodes himself), and with a "gritty" aesthetic that was out of step with modern television. The Chibnall era's "slop" is the production model as much as the stories.

- **RTD's return (2023-present)** is a mixed bag. RTD's "Flux" (2021, Chibnall's final season) was widely criticized (the "slop" of the Chibnall era). RTD's return has been generally well-received — the 60th anniversary specials (2023) were a return to form. But RTD's 2024 season (the "flux" of RTD's return) has been criticized for its pacing and tone. The "slop" of RTD's return is the "flux" — the inconsistency.

## What Works (Both Eras)

- **The best classic Who:** "Genesis of the Daleks" (Holmes, 1975), "The Talons of Weng-Chiang" (Holmes, 1977), "The Caves of Androzani" (Holmes, 1984), "City of Death" (Adams, 1979), "The Aztecs" (Whitaker, 1964), "The Crusade" (Whitaker, 1965).

- **The best modern Who:** "Blink" (Moffat, 2007), "The Empty Child"/"The Doctor Dances" (Moffat, 2005), "Silence in the Library"/"Forest of the Dead" (Moffat, 2008), "Human Nature"/"The Family of Blood" (Cornell, 2007), "The Day of the Doctor" (Moffat, 2013), "The Stolen Earth"/"Journey's End" (Moffat, 2008), "Vincent and the Doctor" (Moffat, 2010), "The Girl in the Fireplace" (Moffat, 2006).

## What Fails

- **Classic failure:** The late 1980s (seasons 24-26) — the Seventh Doctor's "master manipulator" era, the convoluted scripts, the budget cuts, the BBC's lack of interest.
- **Modern failure:** The Chibnall era (2018-2022) — the "forgiveness" theme, the "ordinary" companions, the low-budget aesthetic, the self-importance.
- **The "slop" is not a specific story — it's a production model.** The "slop" is when the show loses its sense of wonder, its sense of fun, its sense of audience connection. The "slop" is when the show becomes self-important, plot-heavy, and disconnected from the audience.
