## Roddenberry's Humanism in Detail

- Roddenberry's future was explicitly secular and humanist. He "refused to have any depictions of religion as a positive force." His vision: humanity had outgrown superstition, embraced reason, and built a utopia through science and cooperation. The humanists.uk profile calls him a key 20th-century humanist figure whose Star Trek imagined "a future shaped by equality and shared human endeavor."

- From The Objective Standard: "His vision of the future was one in which humanity widely embraced reason and rejected mysticism." This wasn't just aesthetic — it was ideological. Roddenberry's Star Trek was a polemic for secular humanism dressed as entertainment.

- He was a WWII B-17 pilot (15 combat missions), then LAPD police officer. His worldview was forged in institutions — military, police, then television. He believed in systems, hierarchy, and rational process. This is the "Starfleet" ethos: a utopian bureaucracy where competence and curiosity are the highest values.

## Roddenberry as Showrunner-Auteur

- Roddenberry claimed primary authorship of Star Trek. He wrote 11 TOS episodes directly. He rewrote "The Cage" footage into "The Menagerie" — which led to a Writers Guild arbitration hearing about credit. This is evidence that his authorship was contested even within his own show.

- The Roddenberry production style: he was the "showrunner" decades before the term existed. He controlled the bible, the story outlines, the casting, the tone. He fought NBC censors constantly (the famous "Kirk and Uhura kiss" was NBCresistance to an interracial kiss). He used allegory to smuggle social commentary past censors: the "Let That Be Your Last Battlefield" episode (two aliens, one black on one side/white on the other, at war) was a direct commentary on racism.

- His production techniques: he would write a story outline, assign it to a writer, rewrite their draft extensively, and often take sole credit. This is the "Roddenberry method" — the creator as editor-in-chief, not necessarily the primary writer.

## Roddenberry vs. Censorship

- NBC banned the Kirk-Uhura kiss (1968, "Plato's Stepchildren") — Roddenberry fought it, eventually getting it filmed but with the camera angles minimizing the shot (the kiss is mostly from behind/side). It was one of the first interracial kisses on American television.

- He constantly fought NBC over: racial representation (Uhura, Chekov), political content (the Vietnam War allegory in "A Private Little War"), and religious content (he opposed the "God" episodes where Star Trek encountered a deity — he wanted no supernaturalism in his future).
