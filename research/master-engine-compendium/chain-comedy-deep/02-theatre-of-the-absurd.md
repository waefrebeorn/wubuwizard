## Core Concept

The Theatre of the Absurd (1950s-1960s) is the philosophical foundation of the ~ WuBu ~ aesthetic. The movement's key insight: life has no inherent meaning, and the comedy comes from characters who continue to act as if it does.

## The Chain

### Samuel Beckett (1906-1989)
- "Waiting for Godot" (1953): Two men wait for someone who never comes. The entire play is waiting. Nothing happens. The audience laughs because the characters keep trying despite the meaninglessness.
- "Endgame" (1957): Characters in a post-apocalyptic room, playing games to pass the time. The structure is circular — nothing changes.
- **Lesson for WuBu**: The Old UNC walks through liminal spaces. Nothing happens. He keeps walking. The audience laughs because he keeps trying despite the meaninglessness.

### Eugène Ionesco (1909-1994)
- "Rhinoceros" (1959): Everyone in a town turns into a rhinoceros. One man refuses. The comedy comes from the absurd logic: turning into a rhino is treated as normal.
- "The Bald Soprano" (1950): Two strangers discover they live in the same house, sleep in the same bed, and have the same child. The comedy comes from the characters accepting absurd premises.
- **Lesson for WuBu**: The ~ WuBu ~ world has absurd rules (hallways breathe, vending machines dispense chickens). The characters treat these as normal. That's Ionesco.

### Jean Genet (1910-1986)
- "The Maids" (1947): Two servants play at being their mistress, then plot to kill her. The comedy comes from role-playing and identity confusion.
- **Lesson for WuBu**: The height opposition world is a Genet construction: identity is determined by height, and the roles are arbitrary but treated as absolute.

## The Absurdist Structure (Applied to 10 Seconds)

The absurdist structure for a bumper:
1. **Establish a world with arbitrary rules** (0-3s)
2. **Characters follow the rules seriously** (3-7s)
3. **The rules are revealed as absurd OR the characters never notice** (7-10s)

## Pitfalls
- **Too bleak** — Absurdist comedy is dark, but it's still COMEDY. The Old UNC is deadpan, not depressing. There's a difference between "nothing matters" (bleak) and "nothing matters, so let's walk down this hallway" (funny).
- **No structure** — Absurdist plays have structure. "Waiting for Godot" is not random — it's precisely timed. The bumper needs the same precision.
- **Explaining the absurdism** — Don't have a character say "Life is meaningless." The absurdism should be visible, not stated.
