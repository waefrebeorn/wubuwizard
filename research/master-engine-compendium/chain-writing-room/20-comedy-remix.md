# Lilypad 20/25 — The Comedy Remix: Borrowing Structure Without Stealing Content

# 7-step Kevin Bacon: comedy remix → borrow structure → transform → parody legal framework → new joke from old form

## The Remix Principle

Every comedy format has a structure. A game show has: host, contestants, questions, prizes, audience. A sitcom has: setup, complication, resolution, tag. A horror film has: normal world, inciting incident, escalation, climax, twist.

The remix takes the structure of an existing format and fills it with ~ WuBu ~ content. The audience recognizes the format (which gives them a framework) but the content is surprising (which gives them the joke).

## Why Remix Works

1. **Instant recognition:** The audience already knows the format. You don't have to explain the rules. They see the game show set and they know what's supposed to happen.
2. **Subverted expectations:** Because they know the format, they know what's *supposed* to happen. When something different happens, the gap between expectation and reality is the joke.
3. **Commentary:** Remixing a format lets you comment on the format itself. "Game shows are absurd" is the subtext of every game show bumper.

## The Parody Legal Framework (WuBu Specific)

The ~ WuBu ~ universe operates on parody principles:
- **You can remix any format** — game shows, sitcoms, talk shows, commercials, news, horror, found footage
- **You can remix any IP** — as long as the result is transformative (it comments on or ridicules the original)
- **You cannot simply copy** — the remix must add something new: a joke, a perspective, a twist

The ~ WuBu ~ approach: take the format, keep the structure, replace everything else with ~ WuBu ~ world rules.

## The Remix Formula

1. **Pick a format** (game show, sitcom, infomercial, nature documentary)
2. **Identify the structure** (what are the beats of this format?)
3. **Map ~ WuBu ~ content onto the structure** (the Old UNC is the host, the questions are existential, the prizes are void)
4. **Find the gap** (what's funny about this specific mapping?)
5. **Push the gap** (make the mapping more extreme until it breaks)

## Remix Examples

### Game Show Remix
- **Format structure:** Host asks question → contestant answers → host reacts
- **WuBu content:** Old UNC asks "WHAT IS THE MEANING OF LIFE" → contestant (a teal orb) says nothing → Old UNC nods, "Correct."
- **The gap:** The format expects a wrong answer and a correction. The ~ WuBu ~ version treats silence as the correct answer.

### Sitcom Remix
- **Format structure:** Character has a problem → tries to solve it → makes it worse → learns a lesson
- **WuBu content:** Old UNC notices the hallway is breathing → tries to ignore it → the hallway breathes louder → he accepts it and moves in
- **The gap:** The sitcom expects growth. The ~ WuBu ~ version expects... accommodation to the absurd.

### Infomercial Remix
- **Format structure:** Problem shown → product introduced → before/after → call to action
- **WuBu content:** "Tired of hallways that don't breathe? Try HALLWAY-AID. [Old UNC sprays something. The hallway sighs.] Order now. Operators are not standing by. There are no operators."
- **The gap:** The infomercial promises a solution. The ~ WuBu ~ version promises nothing and delivers less.

## The 10-Second Application

Each bumper is a remix of a format. The format is identified in the first second (visual cues: game show lights, sitcom living room, infomercial split-screen). The remix happens in the next 7 seconds. The button is the gap between the format's expectations and the ~ WuBu ~ reality.

## Pitfalls
- **Remix is just the format** — if you recreate a game show without any ~ WuBu ~ twist, it's not a remix. It's a game show. The twist is the whole point.
- **Format isn't recognizable** — if the audience can't tell what format you're remixing, they can't feel the gap between expectation and reality.
- **Too many formats at once** — one format per bumper. If you're remixing a game show AND a sitcom in the same 10 seconds, it's just chaos.
