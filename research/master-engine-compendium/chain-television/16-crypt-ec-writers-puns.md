## EC Comics History

- **EC Comics** (Educational Comics, later Entertaining Comics) — founded by **William Gaines** (1922-1993). Gaines inherited the company from his father (Maxwell Gaines, who created comic books as an educational medium — "Classics Illustrated"). William Gaines pivoted to horror/crime comics in the early 1950s, creating the "Tales from the Crypt," "The Vault of Horror," "The Haunt of Fear," "Crime SuspenStories," and "Shock SuspenStories" lines.
- **The Senate Hearing (1954)** — the U.S. Senate Subcommittee on Juvenile Delinquency held hearings on comic books. Gaines testified famously, defending comic books and showing horror panels to the senators. The hearing led to the **Comics Code Authority** (a self-censorship body) and the effective death of EC's horror line. Gaines kept publishing "Mad" magazine (which became a magazine, not a comic, to escape the Code).
- **The EC writers** were a legendary group — **Al Feldstein** (the editor who shaped the horror line), **Harvey Kurtzman** (the Mad genius who left to start Trump and Humbug), **Frank Bellamy** (the artist), **Johnny Craig** (the writer/artist), **Al Williamson** (the artist), **Wally Wood** (the artist), **Johnny Reinstat** (the writer). The EC horror line was known for twist endings, moral irony, and gruesome artwork.
- **The Twist Formula** — EC's horror stories had a specific formula: ordinary people, a supernatural/horror element, a twist ending that revealed the character's true nature or punished their sins. This is the same formula as The Twilight Zone (Serling was influenced by EC's twist endings) and Tales from the Crypt (which adapted EC stories and used the same formula).

## Tales from the Crypt Writers (HBO Series)

- **Ellen Woolfolk** — writer/producer, wrote many episodes. Woolfolk was a television writer who had written for "Twin Peaks" and other series. Her episodes were among the best — her style was more literary than the other writers.
- **Michael Reaves** — writer/producer, co-executive producer. Reaves was a television writer who had written for "Star Trek: The Next Generation" and "The Bride of Chucky." His episodes were action-oriented.
- **Randall Serializer** — writer/producer. Serializer was a television writer who had written for "The Simpsons" and other animated shows. His episodes were comedic.
- **Bruce Poe** — writer. Poe was a horror writer who had written for "The Outer Limits" (1995 revival) and "The X-Files." His episodes were horror-oriented.
- **Jeff Converse** — writer. Converse was a comedy writer who had written for "The Simpsons" and other shows. His episodes were comedic.
- **James V. Hart** — writer. Hart was a screenwriter who had written "Hook" (the Peter Pan film) and "Dracula: Dead and Loving It." His episodes were more literary.
- **Larry Cohen** — writer. Cohen was a horror writer/director who had written "The Stuff" and "Special Effects." His episodes were horror-oriented.
- **Mick Garris** — director. Garris was a horror director who had directed "The Stand" miniseries and "Film Condemned." He directed several Tales from the Crypt episodes.
- **Ernest R. Dickerson** — director. Dickerson was a horror director who had directed "Tales from the Crypt" episodes and "Soul Food." He directed the most famous episode — "The Thing from the Future" (S1E3, actually this was Dickerson's episode, the one with the nuclear mutant).

## The Crypt Keeper's Puns as Memetic Elements

- **The puns** were written by the writing staff. The Crypt Keeper's puns were a deliberate choice — they made the horror accessible, they disarmed the audience, and they became the series' most memetic element. The puns are remembered more than the episodes — "Hello, BOY-zos!" "Mummy's the word!" "I'm dying to meet you!" "I've got a bone to pick with you!" "This is a dead-end job!" "You're in for a shock!" "This is a grave situation!" These puns are the Crypt Keeper's "catchphrases" — the memetic elements that spread beyond the show.
- **The Crypt Keeper's laugh** — the signature cackle. John Kassir's laugh was the definitive Crypt Keeper laugh — the gravelly, cackling, menacing-but-comedic laugh. The laugh is a memetic element — it's recognized even by people who haven't seen the show.
- **The Crypt Keeper's appearance** — the decomposing corpse in the rotting crypt. The Crypt Keeper's appearance is a memetic element — the green skin, the tattered clothing, the bare bones, the glowing eyes. The Crypt Keeper is one of the most recognizable television hosts in history.
