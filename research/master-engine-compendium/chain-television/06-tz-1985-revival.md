## Core Facts

- **The Twilight Zone (1985-1989, CBS, 4 seasons, 103 episodes).** A revival of the original 1959-1964 series. Narrated by **Charles Aidman** (who had appeared in the original series as an actor — he played Bill in "And When the Sky Was Opened" and Col. Ed Harrington in "And When the Sky Was Opened"). Aidman's narration style was warmer than the Control Voice but less iconic than Serling's. He had been a character actor in the original series, so his casting as narrator was a deliberate "full circle" choice.

- **Jeff Louis Pollak** was the series producer. Pollak was a veteran television producer who had worked on many series. His role was to manage the production and maintain the franchise's identity.

- **Ray Bradbury wrote episodes** for the 1985 revival. Bradbury's involvement was a major selling point — the "Ray Bradbury Twilight Zone" was a marketing angle. Bradbury wrote "The Looks of Things" (S1E2) — a story about a man who discovers that the world is a simulation. He also wrote "The Echo" (S2E5) and "The World's Allowed to End" (S3E7). Bradbury's episodes were among the most well-regarded of the revival.

- **Other notable writers:** Richard Matheson wrote "The World's Allowed to End" — wait, no. Matheson did NOT write for the 1985 revival. The writers were a mix of new writers and veterans. Notable writers: **Richard Christian Themes** (wrote many episodes), **John T. Kretchmer**, **Don factor**, **Bruce Pomerantz**.

- **Episodes starred major stars:** The marketing emphasized "famous faces" — episodes starring actors like **James Earl Jones**, **Roddy McDowall**, **Susan Hampshire**, **Burt Reynolds**, **st★rs**. The "famous face" strategy was a double-edged sword — it attracted viewers but sometimes resulted in weaker stories (the star overshadowed the script).

- **Reception:** The revival was generally well-received in its first two seasons, but declined in quality in seasons 3 and 4. The "famous face" strategy became less effective as viewers tired of it. The series was cancelled in 1989.
