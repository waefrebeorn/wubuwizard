# PART ONE: LABS — @WuBuLabs (L-1 … L-6)

## THE FORMAT IS A COMPARISON, ALWAYS

From `VOICES.md`:

> **Structure:** "Here's what they do → here's what we do."
> **Cadence:** technical but not dry; enthusiastic about craft; respectful of
> competition.

**So every Labs episode is a split screen: THEIR METHOD on one side, OUR METHOD
on the other, with the same subject in both.** The Engineer's job is to be
*pleased* about the competition and then show a cheaper, stranger version of the
same trick.

**One hard rule:** Labs never names a real person, company or show. The left
panel is always a *composite* drawn in a distinctly different style from ours —
that is how the respect stays honest. The Engineer cites "the seven-step" and
"the anchor" as our own lineage.

**The recurring bit is L-1, Found Room Technique**: four hours and fifty-nine
minutes of found footage, which is literally the length of the research depot's
own raw clip. The channel's most expensive trick is a room that already existed.

# PART TWO: MEDIA SCHOOL — @WuBuMediaSchool (M-1 … M-6)

## THE FORMAT IS THE CODEC, WITH TEETH

Same Colonel, same codec skeleton as Network — **urgent open, item, aside,
non-sequitur, cut, static** — but the item is now an *expose*. The recurring
phrase from the brief is **"you accepted the fiction."**

**The difference from C-1…C-6 is the target.** The Network codec gestures at our
own channels. Media School points at the shape of a story everyone already
believes, and the Colonel takes it apart in eleven seconds without ever saying he
is doing it.

**Respect rule, and it is absolute:** the target is always a *structure*, never a
person or a company. "Everyone already agreed the rules were the point" is a
target. A named outlet is not.

**One rig.** Same set, same hoodie, same timing as the codec. A clip that drifts
out of the codec set is a continuity error on the network, not on this channel.

## WHY THESE SIXTEEN AND NOT SOMETHING EASIER

Labs, Media School and Public Access are the three channels that make the other
six look like they were *made* rather than *assembled*:

- **Labs is the only channel that admits the trick.** Every other channel
  pretends the world is the world. Labs puts a built room above a real corridor
  and says out loud that ours is cheaper. The Engineer's respect for the
  competition is load-bearing — **the left panel is a composite drawn in a
  deliberately different style, and Labs never names a real person, company or
  show.** Trash the competition and the channel is just an ad.

- **Media School is the codec pointed at a story shape instead of at us.** Same
  rig, same timing, same static — so the two codec channels are visibly one
  service — but the item is an expose and the recurring line is *you accepted the
  fiction*.

- **Public Access is the only warm room in the network, and the only one with a
  laugh track.** The laugh fires on the entrances and on nothing else, which is
  the joke: the audience has decided the room is funny and the room is a normal
  room with one very tall man in it.

**Laura's brief rule is enforced in every prompt**: she is shot smaller in frame
and never in front of him, and **every P prompt carries an explicit bar on UNC
smiling, laughing, gesturing or reacting** — because on a channel with a laugh
track and a warm room, that is exactly the thing a model will do to make it
work, and it would end the entire channel.
