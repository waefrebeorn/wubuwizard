#### NODE 12: Situationist International — détournement, culture jamming, spectacle, Guy Debord
- SI: 1957-1972, international org of social revolutionaries — avant-garde artists, intellectuals, political theorists
- Guy Debord = de facto leader and theorist
- Founders: Debord, Asger Jorn, Walter Olmo, Giuseppe Pinot-Gallizio, Piero Simondo, Elena Verrone, Michèle Bernstein
- Methods: Dérive, Détournement, Situlogy (Hypergraphy)
- Détournement: "turning expressions of the capitalist system against itself" — turning slogans/logos against advertisers/political status quo
- "The literary and artistic heritage of humanity should be used for partisan propaganda purposes... We must now push this process to the point of negating the negation" (Debord + Wolman, 1956)
- "There is no Situationist art, only Situationist uses of art"
- Spectacle: "not the domination of the world by images... but the domination of a social interaction mediated by images" (Debord, Society of the Spectacle, 1967)
- "All real relationships having been replaced by that of relationships with commodities"
- Society of the Spectacle: "written, as Guy Debord once put it, 'with the deliberate intention of doing harm to spectacular society'"
- Legacy: influenced Fluxus, Neoism, Mark Divo; punk rock (Malcolm McLaren, Jamie Reid, Factory Records, Feederz); Sex Pistols ("if the secret history of punk rock is situationism then it's so secret nobody told us"); anarchist movements (King Mob, Weathermen, Rebel Worker); Adbusters, Reclaim the Streets
- SLO journal article: "Revolution for the Hell of It: Abbie Hoffman and the Politics of Playful Disrespect" — Debord's spectacle theory explains Hoffman's pranks as counter-spectacle; penthouse levitation, NYSE money drop, Pigasus nomination; humor as counter-spectacle; danger of recuperation
- Source: TheArtStory, Wikipedia, Springer, Historical Materialism, Détournement Wikipedia

#### NODE 14: Walter Benjamin — "Work of Art in Age of Mechanical Reproduction", aura, commentary theory
- 1935 essay: "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction" (Das Kunstwerk im Zeitalter seiner technischen Reproduzierbarkeit)
- Key concepts:
  - AURA: "that which withers in the age of mechanical reproduction is the aura of the work of art" — uniqueness + locale + tradition
  - "unique phenomenon of a distance, however close it may be"
  - Cult value vs exhibition value — art shifts from ritual to exhibition
  - Mechanical reproduction detaches object from domain of tradition; substitutes plurality of copies for unique existence
  - Art emancipated from parasitical dependence on ritual → based on another pole: politics
  - Film: audience takes position of camera; testing/reviewing attitude; critical and receptive attitudes coincide
  - Distracted mass absorbs work of art — architecture model: used/distractedly perceived
  - "The work of art becomes a creation with entirely new functions, among which the one we are conscious of, the artistic function, later may be recognized as incidental"
- Benjamin's thesis: art's political potential in the age of reproduction — distracted criticism as emancipatory
- MIT PDF of full essay available
- YouTube: "Walter Benjamin: The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction" (Then & Now, 225K views)
- Medium: "Notes on Walter Benjamin's... for my ARTS1 students" — aura, cult/exhibition, film perception, politics
- Reddit r/CriticalTheory: relevance today
- Reference for: commentary system theory — aura, reproduction, comment-as-distracted-critique

#### NODE 15: Fredric Jameson — postmodernism, pastiche, late capitalism, cultural commentary
- "Postmodernism, or, The Cultural Logic of Late Capitalism" (1991)
- Jameson defines postmodernism as cultural system of global, financialized stage of capitalist society
- "Crisis of historicity," "waning of affect," prevalence of pastiche
- Postmodernism as mass-culture driven by capitalism, pervades every aspect of daily life
- Totalizing forces + fragmentation: capitalism/culture expanded into everything, incorporating nature and human subconscious
- Traditional independent self (autonomous ego, solitary rebel, genius) no longer exists
- Modern groups ignore history, rarely challenge capitalist market, distract from larger economic struggles
- "Pastiche" = practice of randomly mixing older styles, genres, codes — replaces true personal artistic style
- "Pastiche Eclipses Parody" — disappearance of the individual
- Source: Amazon, New Left Review, Wikipedia, YouTube, Reddit r/CriticalTheory

#### NODE 22: Yuval Noah Harari deep — Sapiens, "Brief History of Humankind", dataism, future narratives
- "Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind" (2011 Hebrew, 2014 English) — based on 20 lectures to undergraduate world history class
- Themes: free will, consciousness, intelligence, happiness, suffering, role of storytelling in human evolution
- "Cognitive revolution" ~70,000 years ago — Homo sapiens supplanted Neanderthals, developed language + structured societies
- "Homo Deus: A Brief History of Tomorrow" (2016) — future of Homo sapiens; happiness, immortality, God-like powers
- DATAISM: philosophy/mindset that worships big data; entire universe as flows of data; all organisms as biochemical data processors; humanity's "cosmic vocation" = create all-knowing, all-powerful data processor that understands us better than we understand ourselves
- "Homo sapiens is an obsolete algorithm" (Harari paraphrasing Dataists)

- "Unstoppable Us: How Humans Took Over the World" (2022) — children's version of Sapiens, under 200 pages
- Criticism: Ian Parker (New Yorker 2020) — "Harari did not invent Big History, but updated it with hints of self-help and futurology, as well as a high-altitude, almost nihilistic composure about human suffering"
- C.R. Hallpike: "Sapiens as 'infotainment,' a publishing event to titillate its readers by a wild intellectual ride across the landscape of history, dotted with sensational displays of speculation, and ending with blood-curdling predictions about human destiny"
- "Current Affairs": "The Dangerous Populist Science of Yuval Noah Harari" — sells as nonfiction but narratives hue closer to fiction than fact; "Sapiens by contrast is more like a banana republic dictator"; storytelling empty of science; "science populist"

- Source: Wikipedia, Facebook HebrewU, Instagram, YouTube, Current Affairs critique

#### NODE 23: Paul Ricoeur — time and narrative, narrative identity, hermeneutics, time scales
- "Time and Narrative" (Trois volumes, 1983-1985, University of Chicago Press)
- "healthy circle" between time and narrative: time is humanized to the extent that it portrays temporal experience
- Theoretical model of this circle using Augustine's theory of time + Aristotle's theory of plot
- Original thesis of the mimetic function of narrative
- THREEFOLD MIMESIS (the core of Time and Narrative):
  - Mimesis 1 (prefiguration): the stage of our experience of time before narrative — the lived, "raw" temporality
  - Mimesis 2 (configuration/emplotment): the act of emplotment — arranging events into a story, configuration of the narrative
  - Mimesis 3 (reconfiguration): the reception/reading of the narrative — the refiguration that transforms our understanding of time
- Narrative identity: "personal identity as narrative identity" — we are the stories we tell about ourselves
- Relationship between historical writing and fiction: both share narrative configuration; Ricoeur explores the boundary
- Volume 1: the aporias of the experience of time (Book 11 of Augustine's Confessions), time and narrative: threefold mimesis
  - "The Eclipse of Narrative" + "Defenses of Narrative" + "Historical Intentionality"
- Reference for: how the master engine uses time scales + narrative identity as its operating logic
- Source: JSTOR, PhilPapers, Brinkley Blog, Cultural Studies Now, H-Commons PDF, Amazon.de

#### NODE 24: Fernand Braudel — longue durée, civilization threefold time, Mediterranean, deep history
- Fernand Braudel (1902-1985) — French historian, key figure in Annales school, leader post-WWII
- Most significant contribution: THREE-TIERED VIEW OF HISTORICAL TIME:
  1. LONGUE DURÉE — environmental time, practically immobile, geo-history; the long term; slow-changing geographic factors, constraints of natural environment on human production + communication
  2. Medium time — economies, societies, cultures; conjunctures (periods of decades or centuries when more profound cultural changes like industrial revolution can take place)
  3. Short time — discrete events (histoire événementielle); the subject of chroniclers + journalists
- "The history of the event (histoire événementielle), the conjuncture, and the longue durée" — Medium article
- "All the human sciences, history included, are contaminated by one another. They speak the same language, or can do so" (Braudel 1958:731)
- Conceived the three-tiered time while in captivity (1944) — 5 years in POW camps in Mainz + Lübeck; phenomenal memory; drafted La Méditerranée et le monde méditerranéen à l'époque de Philippe II (1949)
- "Total history" — relied heavily on quantification + dazzling microstudies of villages and regions
- Major works: "The Mediterranean and the Mediterranean World in the Age of Philip II" (1949); "Civilization and Capitalism, 15th-18th Century"; "The Identity of France" (unfinished, final work)
- Shifted historical focus from political leaders/diplomacy/wars to ordinary people, climate, demography, agriculture, social dynamics
- Mountains vs plains dwellers — different cultures + economic models — as basic feature of Mediterranean history over thousands of years (first volume of The Mediterranean)
- Mentor: Lucien Febvre; taught at University of São Paolo (1935-37); founded Maison des Sciences de l'Homme (1962)
- Britannica: "Braudel's concept of historical time recognizes that human experience occurs on different time scales, each with its own speed and impact, leaving physical and mental traces"
- Reference for: the master engine's time-scale architecture — the three clocks operating simultaneously
- Source: Britannica, Medium (JNNielsen), ResearchGate, Wikipedia (Longue durée), Oxford Reference
